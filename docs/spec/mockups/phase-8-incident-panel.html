The incident page's work panel, and where Gideon sits

Three asks of 19 September: the Chronology tab is bunched, proposed events should be a pop-out that returns you where you were, and Ask Gideon shoves the page aside. They share one cause, so the first section is the rule that fixes all three; the sections after it draw the choices.

0. One rule for the work panel: tabs, and layers over them

Today the Chronology tab holds everything at once (About, Propose events, the Look for box, the proposals, the events, the find hits), and the sheets (event, clip, sync) open above the wall and push it down. The rule:

  • The wall and the strip never move. Nothing opens above them or beside them; everything a person does opens inside the work panel.
  • The panel has tabs (Chronology, Memo, Cameras, Gideon, Details) and layers. A layer is one job opened over the tab: Proposals, Sync, an event being added or edited, a clip being made, Find's hits. One layer at a time.
  • Every layer has the same head: a Back arrow with the tab's name ("‹ Chronology"), the layer's name, and its one main button on the right. Escape is Back.
  • Back returns exactly: the tab you were on, scrolled where it was, the row you had marked, the moment you were watching. Opening a layer remembers those; closing restores them.
  • A layer's citations still drive the wall (a proposal's time plays every camera), so reviewing never means leaving.

What the layer rule does for the other pieces

  • Sync opens as a layer, not above the wall; the tile's Sync and the Cameras tab's Sync open it on that row.
  • An event (add, edit, note, clip) opens as a layer over the Chronology, the row it came from marked; Save returns to the row.
  • A clip (from an event or the strip) opens as a layer; Make returns to where you were, the clip mark on the row.
  • Find opens as a layer with the hits; a hit seeks; Back returns.
  • Memo, Cameras, Details, Gideon stay tabs. Nothing else opens anywhere else.
