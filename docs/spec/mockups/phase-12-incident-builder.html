Phase 12, chapter 7 rethought · a drawing for the maintainer's word · 2026-11-04

The Incidents tab as a builder: the person picks the cameras, the map follows

The ask of 2026-11-04, after v1.199.0 went on the server: the tab is still all over the place, the surety words ("Clear") mean nothing to a user, and the app has enough tools now to help a person build an incident themselves. Keep the layout (the strip on the left, the Reader on the right); move to click-to-select; add a map at the foot of the tab that takes in a camera the moment it is picked; put the accountability for an incident on the person who builds it, and step away from the app's suggestions. Five figures, the words that change, and five choices. The fictional office throughout.

Not seen. The screenshot in the ask did not reach this side; the drawing works from the tab as v1.199.0 built it and from the words of the ask.

Ticking is building. A tick on a lane adds the camera: its row lights, its bar stays bright while the others dim, its path and marker appear on the map below, its still joins the Reader. The Reader becomes Your incident: a name (offered from the street under the markers when the map knows one, else the case and the time), the cameras with one plain line each, two tools, and Make the incident. Unticking takes it out everywhere at once.

The span is the person's. It starts as the ticked cameras' first start to last end and is drawn as one bright column; two handles on the ruler narrow it. The vertical line is the moment: dragging it moves the markers on the map and recuts the stills, so the person can see where everyone was at 20:28:10 before deciding. Play from here opens the Preview at that moment.

The tools only tick. "Tick everyone running at this moment" and "Tick the cameras within 50 m on the map" (the circle, drawn on the small map) are the app's two helpers, and both just tick boxes the person can untick. No list of the app's own ideas sits on the page.

Accountability, in what the app already keeps. An incident records who made it, when, and by which door (how: by hand, the picker, the span, the map's circle, the strip); the audit row "Incident made" carries the same. The new door is "from the strip", and every incident's head and list row say it in words. No new store.

Nothing made for you. The clocks' offer, the paths' suggestion, the forming run and the same day all go. What the app still does by itself is place a camera on the clock when its picture or its path file says where it belongs, because without that there is nothing to tick; a person can still move or take any of those off.

Adding later. A camera that lands after the incident was built is ticked the same way: tick it on the strip, and the Reader offers "Add to Harbour Street stop" when its span overlaps a built incident's column, beside Make the incident. Still a press by the person.

The budget. "8 of 8" in the Reader's head and the lead; the ticks that are left turn dashed and say why on hover. A vehicle's three streams are one lane and one tick, so the eight are cameras, not files.

The order does the sorting, the person does the choosing. From the first tick the lanes sort by how long each overlaps the span, longest first, with the minutes on the head; lanes with no overlap sink and fade. The likely eight are the top eight rows, and each is still a press.

The filmstrip. The moment line cuts a small picture at the end of every shown lane, so a drag along the ruler shows the same scene from every camera that had it; six cameras on one car park are easy to tell from one that was elsewhere. Cut on request for the shown lanes only, never kept.

Its default becomes the cap, and it can never be set below Most cameras in an incident without a plain line: "Set below 8, some cameras of a full incident wait as parked tiles; a workstation decodes this many videos at once." So an office keeps the valve for weak machines, and by default every camera of an incident plays.

What changes. Today the wall plays six and parks the rest as tiles to swap in; with eight the cap, all eight play in whichever view is chosen. The views keep their shapes: Focus gets seven playing film tiles under the large one, Side by side goes to three across from five cameras, the grids fill as drawn. A vehicle's streams are its tiles, so an incident with a unit may show more than eight pictures; the page says "8 cameras, 10 pictures".

What stays. The parked tiles stay in the code for an office that sets the wall lower; the strip under the wall still shows every camera; Follow, the clock and the work panel are untouched. The One reading measure counts cameras, not pictures, as today.

The cost, said plainly. Eight videos decoding at once is the heaviest thing a workstation does in this app; the setting's line says so, and the Admin's Status page could show the wall's size beside the cap. Built in the first release with the cap, since the cap and the wall are one promise: what you build, you see.

The choices

Settled by the ask, and five to decide

Settled by the maintainer's word (2026-11-04). The layout stays (strip left, Reader right). Click to select on the strip. A map at the foot that takes in a camera as it is ticked. The person builds the incident; the app's suggestions go. The surety words go from this tab. An incident holds eight cameras at most (the Admin's "Most cameras in an incident", read everywhere). And a rule for the build: every word on the tab is clean. No label is drawn over another, none is clipped mid-letter: a name that does not fit is cut with an ellipsis and shown whole on hover, a column's tag yields to a neighbour by stepping down a row, the filmstrip and the ticks have their own columns, and the strip is walked at 1366, at 1024 and on a phone before the release with the office's own sizes (70 cameras, three days) in mind.
