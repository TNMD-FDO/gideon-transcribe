Phase 12, chapter 8 redrawn · a drawing for the maintainer's word · 2026-11-06

The Incidents tab as an incident builder and review desk

The maintainer's word: the page has lost its way; it should be a panel that helps a person build incidents, ticking several videos and scrubbing to moments to help decide, a dynamic builder and a review page. Three directions follow, each drawn at the maintainer's window of 1912 by 1034 with a fictional office. Direction 1 is drawn press by press. Everything the app already knows stays behind the drawings: the cameras' clocks, the path files, the near marks, the stills cut at a moment, the cap of eight, the pictures wall of the incident page.

Figure A · what the tab holds today

Seven pieces that arrived one release at a time: the strip, the Reader, the span box, the incidents menu, two tools, a small map

Where it lost its way. The strip is the day, read left to right; the Reader is a column of words on the right; the pictures at the clock line are thumbnails at the ends of lanes; the map is under everything; the tools are two lines of text in the Reader. A person deciding "do these four belong together?" has to read, not look: there is no place where the ticked cameras are seen side by side at one moment, and the review of what is built is a list of rows under the day's reading.

What stays true. Ticks choose, the cap is eight, the span is the person's, the clock line moves everything, the map knows where each camera was, an incident built opens on its own page with the wall. The builder keeps all of it; it changes what is in front of the eyes.

Direction 1 · the desk: timeline, wall, map, your incident

Step 1, the tab opens: the day's cameras as lanes, a wall that waits for ticks, the map with everyone faint, the built incidents to review on the right

The desk in four parts, all in view. Across the top the builder bar: the day, the clock as a readout and scrub, step buttons, the span, and Make the incident, greyed until something is ticked. On the left the timeline, every camera of the day as a lane with a tick, fitted to the busy stretch (a lone camera running for hours folds), the clock line across it. Under the timeline the wall, where ticked cameras show at the clock line as pictures. On the right the map, every camera faint where it was at the clock line, and under it Your incident and Built on this day.

Nothing ticked yet. The wall says what it is for in one line; Your incident says "Tick cameras on the timeline, or press one on the map"; the built incidents are each a row with a small strip of their cameras, Open and On map.

Direction 1 · step 2

Tick two cameras: their pictures fill the wall at the clock line, the map lights them, the span forms, Your incident counts

Tick, and look. A tick on a lane (or a press on a marker on the map) puts the camera on the wall as a tile: its picture at the clock line, its name, how far into its video that is, and untick. The span becomes the two cameras' first start to last end, shown as the bright stretch on the timeline with handles; the map lights the two and dims the rest. The cap reads "2 of 8".

Direction 1 · step 3

Scrub: drag the clock line and every tile and marker moves with it; a ring on the map and on a lane says a third camera was within 50 m; tick it from either

Deciding by looking. Dragging the clock line (or the scrub under the wall, or ‹ ›) cuts every tile's picture at the new moment and moves every marker. A camera that was within 50 m of a ticked one at that moment is ringed on the map and on its lane, and its tile appears faint on the wall with "near Lee Fox · tick"; a press ticks it. Play runs the wall as video for the ticked cameras in step, the way the incident page's wall does, so the person can watch ten seconds before deciding.

Not the clock, not the pictures. A camera with no clock sits in the Not on the clock row under the lanes with a tick; a camera with no picture at the moment shows a blank tile with the reason.

Direction 1 · step 4

Make: a name is offered, the span is the person's, Make the incident opens the page at the span's start; back on the tab the new incident sits under Built on this day

The review side. Built on this day lists each incident as a row: its name, a small strip of its cameras over the day, the count and the span, who built it and how, Open, On map, and Add the ticked while cameras are ticked. A press on a row lights its cameras' lanes and draws its column on the timeline, so what is built and what is being built are seen together; a second press clears it. "N incidents built" in the bar opens the same list for every day.

What goes. The Reader as a column of words, the span box as a row of fields (the span is the handles and the Set under the clock), the two tools as text (they are presses on the wall: "Tick everyone running now", "Tick the near"), the small map under the strip (the map is beside it).

Direction 1 built out · 2026-11-06 · many cameras, and the wall plays video

A day of twenty-two cameras: the timeline scrolls with the ticked lanes held at its top, and the wall plays up to eight videos in step, scrubbed together

The timeline scrolls. Its box keeps its height (eleven lanes in view here, with its own scrollbar); the ticked lanes rise to the top and stay there above a rule, the rest scroll under them, and the ruler, the incident names and the clock line are held, so the lanes that matter never leave view. A day of twenty-two cameras is eight on top and a scroll for the rest; the lane heads keep their ticks, names and minutes.

The wall plays. Each ticked camera is a tile playing its video at the clock, in step with the others, the same player and the same cap of eight as the incident page's wall (the Cameras on the wall setting, which the workstation decodes at once). Under the wall one transport: Play and Pause, 10 s back and forward, the speed, the clock, and the scrub bar across the span; dragging the clock line on the timeline, the scrub, or a tile's own bar moves all of them. One tile carries the sound (its speaker mark; press another's to switch), and a tile's foot says who, how far into its video, and untick. The wall lays out as a grid: two across for two, four across and two rows from five to eight, each tile 16 by 9 so nothing is cropped.

Review, with or without an incident. Nothing is made until Make the incident, so the desk doubles as a place to watch several videos of a day together: tick, scrub, watch, and leave; the ticks, the clock and the layout are remembered per person for the day, so coming back finds the same wall. An incident is made when the person wants the chronology, the memo, the clips and the stills that only an incident carries.

Direction 1 built out · Focus

A press on a tile makes it the focus: the large picture with the seven others in a strip, the sound following it, for watching one camera closely while the rest keep step

Grid and Focus, the incident page's two shapes, on the builder too: Focus for one camera looked at closely with the others in step in a strip, Grid for all at once; a second press on the focus tile returns to the grid. The said line beside the focus tile shows what is being said on that camera at the clock, as the incident page's said band does, so a person reviewing can follow the words without an incident.

Direction 1 built out · the laptop

At 1366 by 768 the same desk: six lanes in view, the wall four across, the map and the panels in the right column

How eight fit on the laptop. The tiles are about 180 by 100 in two rows, the timeline shows six lanes and scrolls, the map is 190 tall, Your incident lists the eight in two columns, and Built on this day has the rest of the right column with a scroll; the page itself never scrolls. On the docked monitor every box is larger and the wall's tiles are over 300 wide.

Direction 2 · the map leads

The map takes the width with the clock in its band; cameras are ticked on the map or in a circle; the wall sits under the map, the lanes under the wall

For a person who thinks in places. The map is the page (as the Map page is), with the clock and the scrub in its top band; ticking happens on markers or with Within a circle; the wall of the ticked cameras runs under the map, the lanes under that, and Your incident is a panel over the map's right edge with the built incidents behind a tab in it. What it costs: the timeline is third, so "who ran when" takes a scroll; and on a day without path files the map is empty ground, which is the day the Incidents tab was built for.

Direction 3 · four steps, one at a time

When, Who, Check, Make: the builder as a guided walk, each step a page-wide panel

For a person who wants to be led. When: a day and a stretch on one ruler. Who: every camera that ran in the stretch as a still with a tick, the near ones marked. Check: the wall and the map at a scrub. Make: the name and the span. What it costs: the parts are not in view together, so a change of mind means a step back, and the review of built incidents is a separate list; the desk of Direction 1 holds the same four things at once.

The build's recommendation is Direction 1. It answers the maintainer's word directly: tick several videos, scrub to a moment, and see them side by side with the map before deciding; and it puts review beside building, since what is built lights up on the same timeline. The pieces exist: the lanes and their ticks, the clock line, the stills cut at a moment, the near marks, the map's pick mode, the wall's tiles from the incident page. No migration, no setting; the page is rearranged around the wall.
