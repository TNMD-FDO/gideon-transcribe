Phase 12, chapter 6 (the Map tab) · a drawing for the maintainer's word · 2026-11-05

The map is too important to squish: three ways to give it the window

After v1.208.1 the Map tab still shares its height with everything above it: the case head, the state pills, the One reading card, the tab bar, the bar, and the strip under the map. On an office case, in the maintainer's window of 1912 by 1034, that leaves the map under half the height. Every figure here is drawn at that window with an office-shaped case (four days of path files, two state pills, the One reading card), and each says in one number how tall the map becomes. The window still decides every shape, never the monitor. Fictional office throughout.

Direction A · the map is the page while you are on it

The case head folds to one band on the Map tab; the bar and the strip ride on the map: 918 for the map

The band. On the Map tab alone, the case head, its state pills and the One reading card fold into one 44 px band: the case name, the tabs as they are, and "2 need you ▾" for the pills; a press on the band opens the full head over the map, and every other tab keeps the head as it is today. Nothing is lost, it is one press further on this tab only.

The bar rides on the map, a translucent strip along the map's top edge, with the same controls as today: Play, the steps, Speed, the clock, Fit shown, More, Day (a menu now, never chips that wrap), and the ?. The Show box sits under it in the corner as today.

The strip rides on the map's foot, a translucent band of six compact lanes with the ruler and the playhead, and Lanes ▾ folds it to the ruler alone (26 px) when the ground matters more; Show all opens the rest over the map, scrolling inside the band. The list panel is the one of v1.208.0, inside the right edge.

What it costs: the markers under the bands are seen through them (88 % surface), and a long strip scrolls inside its band rather than pushing the page. The map's own height is the window's, 918 here, 666 on the laptop (Figure E).

Direction A, amended 2026-11-05 · Show always in view, the lanes carry the names

Show and the Events ticks sit under the play controls for good, and the strip's lane heads are the list: one place for the cameras, 836 for the map

Show, always. The top band on the map is two rows: the play controls, then Show (Markers only, Trails, Whole paths, faint paths under) and the Events ticks, in view for good; the box in the map's corner goes. The second row costs 32 px.

One place for the cameras. The names panel goes; the strip's lane heads are the list: the tick (ticked is drawn), the colour dot, the officer's name as the path file gives it, a vehicle's speed while it moves, and the ▸ that opens the camera's reading (unit, kind, the clock's surety, Open a tile, Play here, Fly to, Readings, Open the recording, Replace file, Remove), which opens as a card over the map beside the lane. A camera out of view reads faint on its head; the band's own line says "22 of 22 shown · 9 in view · all · none". The folds (Not on the clock, Other days, No path file, Waiting for their video) are rows under the lanes, closed until pressed. The circle's answer takes the same lanes: the cameras that passed inside come to the top with their spans marked.

The band's height. Six lanes of 20 px, the ruler, the heads' line and the folds' line: 172 px on the map's foot, scrolling inside when Show all opens the rest; Lanes ▾ folds it to the ruler. The map keeps 836 of the window and the ground under the bands shows through them.

On the laptop the same shape gives the map 584, against 255 today.

Direction A, amended again 2026-11-05 · the whole timeline in view

Every lane, always: the lanes stand beside the map in a column of their own, the map keeps the window's height

The column. The timeline leaves the map's foot and stands beside it: every camera of the day as a lane, all of them, the ruler across the top and the playhead running down through every lane, the heads as the list (tick, colour, name, speed, ▸), the folds as rows under the last lane, and the band's line ("22 of 22 shown · 9 in view · all · none") at its head. Nothing is folded, nothing scrolls until a day has more lanes than the window has height (over forty at 1034), and then the column scrolls inside itself with the ruler held at its top.

The split follows the window. The column takes 36 % of the width (690 px here, 2 px short of 1,000 on a maximised ultrawide), and its edge drags, remembered per person. The map takes the rest at the full height of the window: 1,030 by 918 here, with only the two-row band on its top edge. A press on a lane or a head lights the camera on the map as the list's rows do today.

On the laptop (Figure I) the column is 480 wide: the day's six hours at 50 px an hour, which still shows every span, gap and event mark; the map keeps 700 by 666.

The other way · every lane under the map

The band at the map's foot grows to hold every lane at 16 px, over the map

What it gives: the timeline at the map's full width, so an hour is 230 px. What it costs: twenty-two lanes at 16 px with the ruler and the folds take 430 px of the map's foot, over the ground; the names run at the smallest size the stylesheet allows and the rows nearly touch, which is the bunching the maintainer ruled out; and a day of forty cameras covers the map entirely. Drawn so the choice is seen, not recommended.

Direction B · a page of its own

The map opens on its own page, as an incident does: 978 for the map

The page. The Map tab stays in the case bar and opens a page of its own, as Incidents open an incident: a 44 px head with ‹ Back to the case, the case name, and the count of cameras; everything under it is the map with Direction A's bands. The rail stays.

What it gives: the most height of the three, and a page that can live in a second browser window beside the case on a wide desk. What it costs: the case's tabs are a press away rather than in view, and On the map from a note or a hit lands on a page instead of a tab (the address keeps working).

Direction C · keep the page, fold what is not the map

The One reading card and the pills fold to a line, the strip to a drawer: 724 for the map

The least change. The case page keeps its head and tabs. On the Map tab the One reading card and the state pills fold into one line ("One reading · 0.7 of 120 hours · 2 need you ▾", 24 px), the bar stays above the frame with Day as a menu, and the strip becomes a drawer: the ruler alone at the frame's foot with the playhead, Lanes ▾ sliding the six lanes up over the map's foot when wanted.

What it gives: 724 here, with the page looking as it does on every other tab. What it costs: the lanes are hidden until asked for, so the camera's span and events are a press away, and the map is still 200 short of Direction A.

The build's recommendation is Direction A as amended twice: Show under the play controls for good, the lanes carrying the names, and every lane in view in a column beside the map, so the map never shares its height with the timeline and the timeline never covers the ground. It keeps the Map a tab of the case, with the case's tabs in view, and gives the map all but 100 px of the window at every size; everything the map needs rides on the map, as it does in any map application the office already knows, and the case head is one press away on this tab only. Direction B gives 60 px more for a page that leaves the case; Direction C keeps today's page and gives 200 px less. No migration, no setting; the bands are the same pieces positioned over the map; the band is a class on the case page while the Map tab is open.
