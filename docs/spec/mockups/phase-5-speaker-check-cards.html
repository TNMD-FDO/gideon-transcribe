One card per speaker, and the merge has to earn its button

When a share of one label's lines read as another speaker's, the check says so once, as a card on that label: how many lines, which speaker they read as, the share as a bar, three moments that read as the other speaker and one that reads as its own, so the ear compares. The small amber pill on the speaker's own card names the finding where the person already looks.

Two kinds of card. A card clears the bar when a clear majority of the label's lines read as one other speaker and the voice step heard that speaker inside many of them; only then is Merge the primary button, since merging against the voice is how a real person vanishes. Below the bar the card describes: it says what the words suggest and what else it could mean, asks for a listen, and keeps Merge as a quiet "after listening" button. None of today's three cards clears the bar; the dashed example shows one that does.

Confidence comes from the voice step and nothing else, in three plain states: two voices heard, one voice heard (the labelled one), or silent. The blind second reading is gone: on this run it backed 1 of 179 and overruled the voice 18 times.

Then the stretches, each a card

What remains after the merges is grouped into runs of the same move close together: one card per run with its span, its line count, the check's reason in a sentence, one moment to hear, and Accept the stretch. The real run had 70 such runs before any merge; with Speaker 5 and 6 folded into Speaker 2, most of them vanish, since they were the same mislabelling seen line by line.

The voice-backed cards come first and get the primary button. A card where the voice disagrees is drawn last, says so, and asks for a listen. "Accept every voice-backed stretch" replaces Accept all, so the one-press path never moves what the voice argued against.

Undo at the top reverses the last merge, as today, and Check again reads the merged transcript afresh.
