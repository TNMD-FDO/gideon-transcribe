The Case remembers: voice prints, Sounds like, the chain and Unlearn

Drawn 2026-10-20 for the maintainer to pick before the voice plan's fourth release is built (ADR 0021; Phase 5 chapter 2 comes into force). Every name, call and line here is made up.

Two rules from 2026-10-19 shape it. The recording's Speakers page is the one place a proposal is accepted or declined, and a Sounds like is a proposal, so it sits on the card with the voice under the ear (figures 1 and 3). The Case's Speakers tab is an overview and the home of Unlearn: what the case knows of each voice, what waits and where, and the chain that Unlearn would undo (figures 2 and 4). Nothing on the tab asks a question the card has not already asked.

What a print is. One vector per Person per Case, learnt only from names and matches people accept, kept as a field of the Person, deleted with the Person, with Unlearn, or when the Admin's switch goes off. Never logged, exported, backed up beyond the database dump, or sent anywhere. The Call system has no print. "Sounds like", never "is"; only high and medium are ever shown, and a print taught by one recording rates medium at best.

What the card says, and when
A Sounds like line appears on an unnamed card when the one run matched its voice to a Person of the case
  • Her voice, beside this one. Preview plays the Person from the recordings that taught her print, as the picker's Preview does; the card's own samples are the voice in question. Nothing to go and find.
  • The rating is the weakest link of the print's own confidence, this recording's calibration and the stretch's length: high or medium. Low is never shown; the card stays plain.
  • Accept is one press and the button says what it does. Behind it the app records two facts apart: this voice is grouped with hers, and this Speaker is this Person, so a wrong name is one rename and a wrong grouping is one Unlearn.
  • Not her dismisses the match for this call; the print is not taught by a refusal. Refused three times for one Person, the tab raises the dismissal line (figure 2).
  • Name or merge... stays below: the picker's rows carry the same sounds like pill on Dana, lit first, so either path names her.
  • Where it lands. Accepting here names the Speaker on this call and the Case tab counts it under Dana; nothing is named on any other call.

One new column, Voice. Under each Person: the print's state (from N recordings, no print yet, or unlearnt and when), a Preview of the voice, the chain in one muted line, and what waits, each a link to the call's Speakers page where the card decides it. The tab never carries Accept.

The chain is the record Unlearn reads. "Named by hand" lines stay through an Unlearn; "matched by voice" lines are what it undoes. It is shown here always, and again inside the Unlearn box (figure 4), so nobody presses it blind.

The dismissal line appears under a Person whose matches were turned down three times in a month, in words, with the way out. The Side alarm is a block of its own because it is about a rule of the case, not a Person's print; it still decides on the call.

Decided so far per source is the measure the plan judges each release by; the per-person table is the one the dismissal line reads from. Both are counts and never a word of a recording.

Nothing else on the tab moves. The Side rule's form, Add person, the Unnamed speakers list and the row's Rename, edit, merge and delete are as they are; unlearn joins that summary on a Person who has a print.

A conflict proposes nothing. Two prints both claim the voice; the card plays each Person from the calls that taught them and offers three answers. Picking one names the Speaker and teaches that print; Neither dismisses both for this call. The tab shows the conflict under both People (figure 2) and points here.

The Side rule's alarm is the one place a named card carries a voice line. "Not Dana on this call..." opens the picker for the whole side, as Name or merge does, and the case rule keeps naming the other calls; "It is Dana" keeps the name and teaches her print from this call. Nothing is renamed by the voice alone.

Unlearn is the row's last, danger button, shown only on a Person with a print, with the chain above it. The box lists each recording by what happens to it, taken from the chain, so what is pressed is what is read.

After. The tab's Voice column reads "voice unlearnt 16 Oct by J. Park" (figure 2, Lee Ortiz). On the returned speakers' cards a quiet pill, "was Dana Reyes, by voice", stays until the speaker is named, and the samples sit where they were. Deleting the Person deletes the print with them, as Phase 2 has it.

Remember voices across a case
Whether a person's voice print is kept inside a case, learnt from the names and matches people accept, so a later recording in the case can say "sounds like" that person. Content: never logged, exported, or shared across cases. Needs Folder management and the Speaker check.
On
Turning this off deletes every voice print the office holds: 11 people across 3 cases. The names stay; only the voices are forgotten. The Case tabs say "voices were forgotten on " until a print is learnt again.

One switch, Admin only, in the Speakers settings beside the voice step's rows, as the 2026-10-19 note asked: no second place to turn voices on or off. Greyed with the reason while Folder management or the Speaker check is Off.

Off is honest and loud. The warning names the count before the switch is thrown; the prints go at once, the names stay, and the Case tabs say so. Turning it on again starts from nothing: prints are learnt from the next names and matches people accept.

What this is not

Left as it is, and what is kept

Nothing is named by the voice alone. Every Sounds like, conflict and Side alarm is accepted by a person on the call's card. No Accept all for names or matches, ever.

Nothing crosses a case. A print lives on a Person of one case and is matched inside that case only. The Call system has no print anywhere.

What is stored. One vector per Person per case, a field on the Person; the chain is the audit rows the app already writes for a name and a match (counts and who, never a word or a vector); the match's rating and the run that made it, on the proposal, as the check's proposals already carry theirs.

Phones. The card's Sounds like block wraps as the hand-over does; the tab's table stacks its columns as the case page already does under 600.
