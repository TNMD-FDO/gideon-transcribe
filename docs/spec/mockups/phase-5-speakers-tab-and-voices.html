The Case's Speakers tab and the Voices place, as people rather than rows: who is in this case, what the app knows of their voice, and what still needs you

Drawn 2026-10-22 at the maintainer's word that the tab should look, feel and act more intuitively than the list it is, and that the Voices place should follow. Drawn from the user's seat: a lawyer or paralegal opens the tab to answer three questions, who is in this case, is anything waiting on me, and can I trust what the app says about a voice. Every name, case and recording here is made up.

What does not move. The recording's Speakers page stays the one place a proposal is accepted or declined; this tab points at it and never decides. The tab keeps everything it does today (rename, role and notes, merge, delete, Unlearn, Keep for the unit, the Side rule, Add person, the unnamed speakers, Decided so far) and loses nothing; it changes where those sit and how they read. The Voices place shows no line, no time and no case the reader may not open, as before. The Preview opens as the panel of the companion drawing, never as a card over the page.

What needs you, first. The strip at the top is the review workflow in four cards: voices still to name (with the button that opens that recording's guided Speakers page), Sounds like waiting (to the card where it is decided), the Side rule's alarm, and the Side rule itself as a quiet statement with "change". Nothing is decided here; every button goes to the place that decides. When nothing waits, the strip is one calm line: "Nothing waits on you in this case."

One card per person, in the same order every time: anyone with something waiting first, then by how much they are heard. Each card reads top to bottom: who (name, role), the voice (play, how many recordings taught it, the clock verdict of the sample, the unit's book as one pill), where they appear (a chip per recording that opens it at their first line), the chain in one line (named by hand here, by voice there, accepted by whom), what waits, and the counts. The chips filter the grid; "Add a person" opens the small form in place.

Every action is in one menu behind the card's three dots: Rename everywhere, Role and notes, Merge into..., then apart from them Keep or Stop keeping for the unit, Unlearn their voice and Delete from this case, with the same confirm boxes as today. A card never shows six forms at once, and a destructive act is never one press.

The Preview is the right column. Play on a card opens the person's voice beside the cards, as the companion drawing has it: the samples, the lines around the moment, a tab per recording, the clock pill. The column is empty until a play is pressed and gives itself back on Close.

The same card, the book's facts. Name and role; the voice with how many cases and recordings taught it and the rating rule as a pill (well taught, medium at best); who kept it from which case, or "a case you may not open"; the checks' counts and the last time it was heard; the turned-down call-out when a print keeps being wrong. The menu holds Hear them and Forget, with the confirm box as it is today. A forgotten voice dims and says how to keep it again.

The month at the top replaces the two tables at the foot: five numbers a unit lead can read in a glance, and the chooser of units for an Admin as chips. The Decided-by-month table stays under a "Numbers" fold for anyone who wants the history.

The Preview here plays only what the reader may open, as the rule has always been; the heading says how many of the recordings that taught the book are open to them.

The tab is an overview plus Unlearn. No proposal is accepted or declined here. Every "waiting" line and every strip button opens the recording's Speakers page at the card in question. Unlearn, Keep and the Side rule stay here because they are facts about the case, not about one call.
Nothing is lost from today's tab. Rename, role and notes, merge, delete, Unlearn, Keep and Stop keeping, the Side rule, Add person, Unnamed speakers and Decided so far are all present; six forms became one menu, two tables became a fold and a line per card, the unnamed list became the first card of the strip.
The Voices place follows the tab. One card design in both places, so a person seen in a case is recognised in the book; the book's card carries the book's facts (cases, kept by, checks) and never a line, a time or a case the reader may not open.
The Preview is one panel everywhere. Play on either page opens the companion drawing's panel in the right column, with the clock verdict; on a phone, the sheet.
