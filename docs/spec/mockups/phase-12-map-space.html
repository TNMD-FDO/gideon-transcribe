Phase 12, chapter 6 (the Map tab) · a drawing for the maintainer's word · 2026-11-05

The Map tab: the same pieces, the space used

The ask: "For the map page, I like what it is, but we need to make better use of the space." So nothing is taken away and nothing new is invented: every control, the list, the strip, the cards and the circle stay as they are. What changes is where they sit, so that the map, the list and the strip are all in view at once on an office laptop, and the docked ultrawide monitor gets a map as tall as the window instead of a capped one with an empty column beside it. Two directions are drawn, each at 1366 by 768 (the laptop) and at 2560 by 1200 (a window maximised on the docked monitor), with the ten choices at the end. The window decides, never the monitor: every shape here follows the browser window's own width and height, so a person who keeps a modest window on a wide screen gets the laptop shape (Figure G), and the map grows and shrinks with the window as they resize it. Fictional office throughout. Decided 2026-11-05: Direction 1, the frame growing with the window without a cap.

What is right about it and stays: one clock for every camera, the list beside the map with its ticks and folds, the strip's lanes under it, the event and camera cards, the circle, the picture, Larger map and full screen.

What wastes the space: three rows of controls where one fits; an explanation that a person reads once; the list column kept at 320 wide and as tall as the map whatever it holds; the map's height capped at 640 instead of taking the window; and the strip placed under everything, so the thing that says when is never beside the thing that says where.

Direction 1 · the laptop

One bar, then one frame: the map takes the window's height, the list stands inside the frame, the strip is its foot

The bar. Play, the steps, the event steps, the speed, the clock with its date under, Fit shown, and a More menu for Note here, Note on all shown, Within a circle, Save a picture and Tidy names, which a person reaches for now and then, not every minute. The Show choice (Markers only, Trails, Whole paths) and the Events ticks move onto the map's top-left corner as a small overlay with a fold, so they sit where their effect is. The explanation sentence goes behind a ? at the bar's end.

The frame. One box from the bar down: the map fills it, the list is a panel inside its right edge (the same panel Larger map draws today, now always there, 300 wide, folding to a chip that says "5 of 5 shown"), and the strip is the frame's foot at the map's full width, the clock line running up through the map's edge so the two read as one. The frame is as tall as the window leaves it, measured again whenever the window is resized: on this laptop 420 for the map and 110 for the strip, all in view with nothing to scroll; in a taller window the map is taller.

Days sit on the bar as chips only when the case has more than one day; a single day reads in the clock's date line, as it does today. Larger map is no longer needed, since the map already has the window; the ⤢ corner keeps full screen. Nothing grows because the screen is wide: only the window's own size counts.

Nothing moves on the case page itself: the head, the state pills and the One reading card stay as they are on every tab.

Direction 1 · a window maximised on the docked monitor

At 2560 by 1200 the frame grows with the window, the list panel's folds open, and the cards take a column of their own

The third column. When the window itself is 2200 or wider the cards leave the map: a camera's reading (its row opened: unit, wearer, how it sits on the clock, Play here, Add or Replace its path file) and an event's card (what, where, until when, Play here, Offer to the chronology, previous and next) draw in a column of their own on the right, 380 wide, so they never cover the map and a long list of readings has height to use. On the laptop they stay as the cards over the map, as today.

The list panel has the height to open its folds (Never inside, Not on the clock, Other days, No path file, Waiting for their video) without scrolling, and the map keeps 1800 by 940 of ground.

Direction 2 · the laptop

The list is the strip's heads: the map takes the whole width, and the cameras are listed once, on their own lanes

One list, not two. Today a camera is named twice: in the list column and on its lane's head. Here the lane's head is its row: the tick, the colour dot, the name, the unit or the speed while playing, and the ▸ that opens its reading; the lanes run beside the heads as they do today. The folds (Never inside, Not on the clock, Other days, No path file, Waiting for their video) are rows under the lanes, closed by default. The map gets the 320 the list column had, so at 1366 the ground is 1150 wide.

The cards (a camera's reading, an event's card, the circle's list) open as a panel over the map's right edge, as the circle's list does today, and close on Escape or a press outside.

The cost. With many cameras on a day the strip is tall (24 px a lane; twelve lanes show and Show all the rest, as the Incidents tab does), and "in view" grouping, which the list does by moving rows between In view and Out of view, becomes a mark on the head instead (a faint head for a camera out of view). The bar and the Show overlay are the same as Direction 1.

Direction 2 · a window maximised on the docked monitor

At 2560 by 1200: the map and the strip, and the cards in their own column

The same third column as Direction 1 for the cards. The map is 2100 wide by 880 tall; the strip under it has the width to show every minute of a six-hour day at better than a pixel a second.

Which fits the office better comes down to one question: is the list column worth 320 of the map's width? Direction 1 keeps it (as a panel that folds); Direction 2 folds it into the strip.

Figure G · a modest window on the docked monitor

A 1600 by 900 window on the ultrawide gets the laptop shape: the window decides, not the screen

What the rules are. The cards' column only when the window is 2200 or wider; the frame's height is what the window leaves, measured again on every resize; full screen is a press. A person who likes a small window beside their notes keeps a small map, and nothing is decided from the monitor.

What stays the same at every size: the bar, the Show overlay, the list panel, the strip as the frame's foot. Only the proportions follow the window.

The bar, close up

The three rows become one, with More for the occasional presses and the Show choice on the map itself

Always on the bar: Play, −10 s, +10 s, ‹ event, event ›, Speed, the clock, Fit shown. These are pressed every minute.

Behind More: Note here, Note on all shown, Within a circle, Save a picture, Tidy names. Each keeps its words and its greyed state with the reason; the menu closes on a press and on Escape. A control the office presses often can be promoted to the bar later, since it is one line of markup.

The Show overlay in the map's corner: folded it reads "Trails · 6 kinds of event"; open it is today's segment and the six ticks. Remembered per person as today.

Both directions are the same code moved: the bar is today's three rows in one flex row with a menu; the frame is a grid of two rows (the map and the strip) with the list panel positioned the way Larger map already positions it; the height is one measure from the window, taken again on resize; the third column is a media query on the window's width at 2200; the monitor is never asked. No migration, no setting, nothing new kept; map.json is untouched. One release, walked at 1366 by 768 and at 2560 and 3440 wide, light and dark. The build's recommendation is Direction 1, because it keeps every piece where a person who knows the tab today will look for it.
