The Incidents tab redesigned, with everything built so far in hand

The ask of 2026-11-04: "redesign the Incidents tab considering everything we have built". The tab grew in six layers over five weeks (the clocks' offer, the strip, the same day, the stills, Not on the clock, the suggestions from paths), each sound alone and together a page that speaks four dialects. Below: what the tab holds today and where it hurts, then three directions drawn as whole pages, the ideas every direction shares, and a recommendation. The fictional office throughout.

1 · The lead is a paragraph. Four sentences of definitions before anything can be done, written for the first visit and read on every one.

2 · The lanes are named by file. "Officer1_20251106…" on every lane and in the legend, while the Map tab and the suggestion cards already know the officers by name from their path files. The bars are too thin to carry a word on a six-hour day.

3 · Three thin lanes say little. The notes lane is a "+13" chip, the incidents lane a cut label, the Suggested row a dot; each is a different idiom for "something is here".

4 · Four kinds of card, four vocabularies. Offer / Add it / Not these; a numbered suggestion / Make an incident / Choose myself / On the map / Not this; Same day / Add to the forming one / Its own matter; and the forming card of v1.180.0 still in the code. The person has to learn that these are all the one thing: the app thinks some videos go together.

5 · The incidents come last. The tab is named Incidents, and the incidents the office made sit at the foot as a table with seven columns.

6 · Not on the clock is a page of its own. Under everything, with its own rows and presses, though it is the same day's videos as the strip's.

Order of use. What the office made comes first, as cards with a still per camera and the facts the table held (the One sitting figure stays, as a phrase). Then one Suggested block where every kind of suggestion the app can make (a camera inside an incident's span by the clocks, cameras together by their paths, overlapping videos with notes, the same day) is a card of one shape: a number, a headline that names the officers, one source pill, one line of why, the stills, and the same three presses: the primary (Make an incident, or Add to name), Look, and Not this.

Look. One press for "show me": it opens the strip below, lit to those cameras, and offers On the map beside it. The picker and Choose myself go behind the primary press: Make an incident opens the New incident box with the cameras ticked, where the person can still change the ticks, which is what Choose myself did.

The strip is a tool, folded. The day's timeline, Not on the clock and the day chips live under one fold, opened by Look or by hand, drawn as Direction B draws it. The tab opens short; a case with no incidents and no suggestions says so in one line with New incident.

The strip grows up. The lanes are named by the officer (from the path file, else the unit, else the file), one lane per camera, a vehicle's streams as one lane; the ruler fits the day's recordings so the bars have width; the notes are marks on their own lanes, no notes lane. An incident is a shaded column across every lane it covers, with its name on top; a suggestion is a dashed column with its number; a camera that is suggested into an incident is lit inside the incident's column. The thin lanes of today (notes, incidents, Suggested) are gone: the things are drawn where they happen.

One panel reads what you press. Press a suggestion's column and the panel on the right says who, when, why, the stills, a small map of where they were at that span (the Map tab's drawing, read-only, with Open the map), and the two presses. Press an incident's column and it reads the incident (cameras, span, events, One sitting, Open). Press a lane and it reads the camera (its clock and surety, its notes, Play here, On the map). Press nothing and it reads the day. One place to read, instead of cards of four kinds.

Not on the clock is the strip's last row: the clockless videos named, with Place… opening today's panel as a sheet. The day chips stay in the lead. The incidents table goes; the day panel lists the incidents with Open, and a day chip carries its incident count.

One list, in the order of doing. The incidents made, the suggestions, the videos not on the clock, and the count of videos in no incident with New incident: every item is one row with one primary press. It reads like a work queue, which is what the tab is for an office that has 27 videos and no incident yet.

The evidence follows the choice. Choosing a row shows its panel on the right (who, why, stills, a small map, the presses) and the day's strip under it with that row's cameras lit; an incident row shows its summary and its span on the strip; a Not-on-the-clock row shows the file's facts, the sound line and the place presses with its still. Nothing on the left needs a card.

The cost. The strip is narrower (about 760 px at 1366), so a day of seventy cameras scrolls inside its box; under 1024 the list goes on top and the panel under it, as the Map tab's list does.

A recommendation, and the choices

Direction B, with Direction A's one card shape inside its panel

Why B. The maintainer asked for the strip on 2026-10-30 ("a timeline view that updates with notes") and it is the one thing on the tab that shows time; today it is starved of names and width and hemmed in by three thin lanes. B gives it the names the path files brought, draws incidents and suggestions where they happen on it, and puts every reading in one panel. A is the simplest and would do well for an office that makes incidents by hand; C is the best work queue but halves the strip. B keeps the timeline the hero and folds A's one vocabulary into its panel. Built in two releases: the lanes, the columns and the panel first; the small map and Not on the clock as a row second.
