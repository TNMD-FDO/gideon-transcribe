The incident page: three ways to lay it out

Second round. Option B is chosen. The Layout menu in its transport gains Side by side, the grid of cameras beside the panel with the strip at the bottom, drawn under option B below. The other options stay for the record.

Mock-ups for review, drawn in the app's own look at a 1200 pixel desktop width with four cameras of a traffic stop. Nothing here is built. Each option keeps every feature of v1.61.0; what changes is where things sit and how much room the pictures get.

What makes the page feel smushed today. The wall shares the width with the work panel, so with four or more cameras each picture is a quarter of half the screen. Three rows of controls (the head, the transport, the tile heads) sit between the pictures and the words. The strip is at the bottom, far from the wall it drives, and the parked tiles take a row of their own. The event box pushes everything down when it opens.

Option A. Stage first, then the story

The wall takes the whole width. The transport sits under it, the strip directly under that, and the Chronology, Memo, Cameras and Details tabs run full width below. Watching happens at the top; reading and adding events happens below, in a natural scroll.

Tidy-ups that go with any option

  • Tile heads lighter. The colour dot, the camera id, one short pill (clock, sound, file, hand, guess), a speaker icon for the sound, Sync, and the three-dot menu. The long pill words move into the pill's hover.
  • Sound is one press. The speaker icon on a tile picks the sound; the Sound from list leaves the transport.
  • Across becomes Layout: Grid, Focus, 2, 3, 4 across, in one menu.
  • Parked cameras live in the strip as thin lanes with Swap in, not as a row of tiles under the transport.
  • The event box is a sheet that opens over the head, never pushing the wall down.
  • The words under a tile in a slightly larger size, with + event on hover as today, and the camera line's + event kept for videos with vision.

The earlier recommendation, for the record

Before the choice: build option A, with option B as its Focus layout and the tidy-ups above. A gives the pictures the whole width, which is what "smushed" is about, and puts the timeline where the eye expects it. B answers the other half of the problem, that a person usually cares about one camera at a time while wanting the rest in view, and it is what makes eight or twelve cameras workable. C keeps today's shape and fixes its worst habits, but it still divides the width between watching and reading, so I would only take it if you want the chronology always beside the pictures.

A, stage first

  • Biggest pictures at every camera count.
  • Timeline under the transport.
  • Reading the chronology means scrolling down.

B, focus

  • One camera large, words readable.
  • Scales to twenty cameras.
  • No side-by-side comparison in this mode.

C, side by side

  • Chronology always in view.
  • Least change from today.
  • Pictures still share the width.
