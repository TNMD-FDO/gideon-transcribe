The note first. Each note is its own block, the office's words in the largest type on the page. Under them, where it rests: the time in bold and whose line, then the line itself quoted in italics as it reads in the transcript, then the writer and the date, small. The notes are grouped under their recording, newest recording first as the Notes page lists them, and an incident's event notes under the incident.

The still. For a note on a camera recording, a still from the playback copy at the moment the note was made, a thumbnail at the left with its time and the camera's name burned in small. It is taken when the document is made and kept nowhere, as the chronology report's stills are. A note at a moment where nothing was said gets its still too, since the picture is often the point of such a note. Audio-only recordings and event notes have no still; their block is the full width.

Everything the table carried stays. The cover line's counts, the writer and date, the kind of note, the running head and the page marking. A note whose line's clock was re-read says so in a small pill after the time, as the pages do. Nothing is said that is not in the case; nothing about what was downloaded goes beyond the one audit row the export already writes.

Cost. One frame per camera note, a fraction of a second each; a case with hundreds of camera notes takes a few seconds longer to download. Over a cap of 300 stills the document says so at the top and leaves the rest without a picture, so a download never runs away.

Two kinds, said so. The people are shown as chips, read-only here, with the role and the nicknames the Speakers tab holds; the chip's link goes to the person's card. Adding a person, a nickname or a role is the Speakers tab's work, in one place, as the maintainer asked. The places and terms are added, given spellings and removed here, as today.

One answer to "what is it for". The three readers under the block say in a line each what reads the list, so nobody has to guess whether a spelling helps recognition (it does not) or whether a nickname reaches Search (it does).

The unit's voices, if wanted. Not drawn: the names of the voices a unit keeps could join recognition for recordings in that unit's cases, as officers' names are said across cases. It is a small change to the recognition list and a line in this block ("and the Trial unit's 6 kept voices"); it waits for the maintainer's word.
