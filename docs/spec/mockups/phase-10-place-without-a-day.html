Placing a recording on the clock before any day exists

The chronology works one day at a time, and a day exists only when a note sits on a recording with a clock. A case whose first note lands on a recording with no clock, which is what accepting a proposed event on a phone call does, has no day, so today the page shows a message that points at a rail it never draws. The drawing puts the rail and Place recordings under the message, lets the first placing happen from there and from the Proposed events tab, and opens the day just made. Made-up case.

What changes. The rail is drawn whenever the case has a recording with a note and no clock, day or no day. Its chips and Place recordings work as they do on a day: a press on a chip opens the place box (figure B), Place recordings the sheet for all of them. The message says what to do instead of pointing at nothing.

What does not. The strip, the ruler and the band stay absent until a day exists; there is nothing to draw them from. A video whose clock was read still places itself.

The first date. With no day to borrow, the box starts from what the file suggests when its name or header carries a date and time (Phase 9 chapter 7's reading, where the rail already shows "Use the file's time"); otherwise the date is empty and must be typed. Today's date is never assumed: a recording is rarely made today. (Choice 1 below.)

After Place the chronology opens on the day just typed, with the note's entry on it (figure C). Nothing else about the place box, the sheet or the clock changes; these are chapter 1's as built, reached from one more place.

On the Proposed events tab the Not yet placed group gains the same Place recordings, opening the sheet for the recordings with proposals and no clock. The words no longer send you to another tab. After Place the tab redraws on the day just made and the entry moves onto it. (Choice 2 below.)

On the Notes tab the same placing opens the day with the note's entry, "placed by hand" on it, and the accepted proposal's origin on its pill. The bar's start is the time typed; a placing by the day alone lists the entry under Time unknown on that day instead.

The cameras place themselves. BWC1-1 and BWC3-1 had their clocks read from the picture, so they are on 6 Nov by themselves, bars and entries, nothing to do. The accepted proposal on BWC3-1 is entry 2 on both tabs: a note on the Notes tab, with its origin; an accepted entry on the Proposed events tab.

The call waits on the rail. Call_20251109_1420 has no clock to read, so on the Notes tab it sits on the rail above the ruler with its note count, and on the Proposed events tab its proposal sits under Not yet placed by its own time. Place recordings on either tab opens the sheet (figure B) for it alone; placed, by date and start or by the day alone, it joins its day on both tabs, and a day that did not exist yet appears on the strip.

Nothing else moves. The rail is per recording, as chapter 1 has it; this drawing only adds it to the no-day state (figure A) and to the Proposed events tab's group. A mixed case already shows it on a day.

Choices

Two things only the maintainer can decide

  1. The first date in the box when there is no day. Recommended: the file's own date and time when it suggests one, else empty and typed. Or: today's date, as a day's box borrows the day shown.
  2. After placing from the Proposed events tab. Recommended: stay on the tab, redrawn on the day just made. Or: go to the Notes tab's chronology on that day, where the note now sits.

Size. One small release, no migration: the day address returns the rail with no day; the chronology draws it with the message; the place box and the sheet take their first date as chosen and open the day placed; the Proposed events tab's group gets the button.
