One column, the page scrolls. Head, the primary button at the full width, the player as one row, the voices, the lanes, the hand-over, the transcript. Nothing is a second page or a sheet; it is the desktop page folded.

The voices swipe sideways. The cards keep their samples and their proposal, but sit in one row that scrolls, so the transcript starts within one screen's height. The card in hand comes first.

The lanes keep their shape. The label moves above its track, the scale is drawn once under them, the hand-over marker stays on its lane. The hand-over row's buttons wrap to their own line.

A line is three rows. Play, time and the labels on the first; the words on the second at the full width; the three sounds and Accept or Leave on the third. A run of lines moving together has its head with Accept all, and each line only Leave this one. The disagreed tint and the pill stay.

The two views are one segmented control at the full width. The fold is one full-width button. The foot keeps Decided so far.

A bottom bar carries Run again, the count left, and Done, so the way out and the way back never scroll away. The one primary stays at the head.

One pill, in the strip's tools, while anything waits. "14 changes proposed", and when a hand-over was found, "· another voice under Speaker 1" on the same pill. It is a button: a press opens the Speakers page. The count goes down as things are settled and the pill goes when nothing waits.

Nothing else on the strip moves. The chips, the count, Manage speakers, hide and fold are as Phase 1 and chapter 1 have them; the Call system chip keeps its grey italics. A line a change would move is not marked in the viewer; the reading stays clean, and the Speakers page is where the proposed version lives.

While the run reads, the pill says "checking the speakers…" and is not a button; the Speakers page's own state line says more.

On a phone the strip takes four rows: the head with the count and fold, the chips, the pill at its full width, then Manage speakers and hide. Folded, it is the head row alone.

What the drawings decide

For chapter 3's "The Speakers page", under Phone and narrow screens, and "The strip"

Under 600 wide the Speakers page is the desktop page in one column with the voices in a row that scrolls sideways, a line in three rows, the views as one segmented control, the fold as one full-width button, and a bottom bar with Run again, the count left and Done. The one primary stays at the head.

The strip's pill carries the count and, when one was found, the hand-over, and is the one way from the recording page to the proposed version. The viewer's lines are not marked. On a phone the strip takes four rows and folds to one.
