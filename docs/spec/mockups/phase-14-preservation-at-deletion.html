The Preservation copy at deletion, and Archived

The second of the plan's three releases. A Preservation copy is made by itself when a case is deleted, by its owner or by the retention clock, and is kept for a number of days an Admin sets, in a new place called Archived. What you have already decided is drawn as decided; what is still open is in the picks. The email to the owner is the release after this one. Every name below is the local demo's made-up case.

Preservation copy at deletion

When a Case is deleted, by its owner, by the Retention policy or by an Admin, its Preservation copy is made first and kept in Archived.

When changed: the next deletion. Off: nothing is made or kept at deletion, and copies already in Archived stay their days.

On Default: On
Preservation copy kept

The days a deleted Case's Preservation copy is kept, counted from the day the Case was deleted. A Case in the Recycle bin always keeps its copy while it waits there.

When changed: the next sweep. A copy already past the new number is removed that night.

The picks

One answer to each, please; my recommendation is marked.

PickThe choicesWhy I recommend it
1. While the copy is made at Delete1a The dialog counts the documents; the person may leave and the case is deleted when its copy is ready, Figure A moment 2 recommended
1b The person must stay on the dialog until it is done		Most cases take seconds either way. The largest on the server would hold 193 documents, and nobody should have to sit and watch that.
2. If the copy cannot be made2a The case is not deleted; the dialog names the document and offers Try again and Delete without a copy, Figure A moment 4 recommended
2b The case is never deleted without a copy
2c The case is deleted anyway and the failure only logged		You said the owner is in charge and the app must not hold a deletion back. 2a keeps both: nothing is lost by accident, and the owner can still delete.
3. A case the clock deletes3a The copy's days count from the day the case went to the Recycle bin, as you described: with both at 30 it never reaches Archived, Figure D middle line
3b The copy's days count from the day the case is wiped, so every copy has its full days in Archived recommended		A clock-deleted case is the one nobody was watching, so its owner is the least likely to have downloaded the copy while it sat in the bin. 3b gives that copy 30 days after the case is truly gone. It is a change from what you said, so it is yours to choose.
4. Who sees a copy in Archived4a Its case's owner, and Admins (audited) recommended
4b Also everyone the case was shared with		The same rule as the Recycle bin, and the copy is the owner's to save.
5. Removing a copy early5a The owner (and an Admin) may press Remove now, behind a confirmation, audited recommended
5b Only the nightly sweep removes a copy		Once it is saved to the client's file, some will want privileged work off the server at once. It is their copy to remove.
6. The switch6a A switch and the number of days, the switch On by default, Figure C recommended
6b The number of days alone; a copy is always made		For your office it is On either way. The app is also for other offices, and one of them may hold that Delete must leave nothing behind.
Said so it is not a surprise: a copy in Archived is a small file in the app's own data folder and is in every Backup; it counts toward nobody's room. If the app cannot make a copy during the nightly sweep, the case still goes to the Recycle bin (it is all still there) and its row offers Make the copy. The reminders in the nightly email and the message to the owner are the next release, not this one. This release changes what "Delete is final" means, so it comes with a short decision record.
