Stacked pips on the map: a press that asks which one

Offered on 2026-11-04 with the knots work and never asked for. A camera's pips mark where something happened on its path: a note, the lights, the siren, the mic, a bookmark. Where a camera stood still, or several stood together, the pips land on one spot. Today a press on the stack opens the top one alone. The drawing gives the press a small list to choose from, the same small-list idiom a vehicle's name tag already has. Three figures and five choices. The fictional office throughout; no real place, name or time.

The list. A press on a spot with more than one pip under it opens a small list beside the stack: a head ("4 at this spot"), then one line per pip in order of time: the time of day, what happened in the words the card uses, and whose camera. A hover lights the line; a press opens that pip's card, Figure C. Escape, a press elsewhere, or a press on the stack again closes the list.

Where it sits. Beside the stack on whichever side has room, kept inside the map pane as the event card is; on a phone it is a sheet at the foot, as the card is.

One pip under the press opens its card at once, as today; the list is only for a stack. A note at a moment the camera had no fix has no pip, as before, and is reached from its lane.
