The voice plan on the pages

Set aside on 2026-10-19. The maintainer found these question cards unintuitive: a question like "is this speaker the same as line 5" needs the person to leave the page and look. The shape that replaces them is phase-5-review-as-reading.html: the transcript with the changes marked in place, the cast with its sound, the hand-over as a marker in the lane. Kept here as the record of what was not chosen.

Four places the voice plan touches, drawn in the app's own look for you to pick before anything is built (ADR 0021). Figures 1 and 2 are the one question: the Speakers page after one run, as one list or as groups. Figures 3 to 5 are the Call system, the Person's two lists, and the Case's Speakers tab. Every name and line here is made up.

One list, four kinds of question, one order: by how many lines a yes settles. A Hand-over that makes a Speaker of 48 lines comes before a one-line move. The kind sits in small capitals over each card so the eye knows what sort of question it is, and the voice's word sits beside it as a pill in the pills' colours: green agrees, red against, grey could not judge.

Every card is one question with yes and no, as v1.129.0 settled. The primary button is filled only where the voice backs the proposal; where the voice is against, both buttons are plain and the card says to listen first. Nothing on this page moves by itself.

Names and Sounds like are cards in the same list. Suggest names stops being a separate button: the run suggests names as it checks lines, and the page says so in its state line. A Sounds like card says plainly that yes does two things, grouping the voice and naming it, and that each can be undone on its own.

The Call system's lines are counted under a fold at the foot and never proposed for anything.

The same questions in four groups, each with its count, so a person who only wants to name people goes straight to Names. The order within a group is by lines settled, as in A.

What it gains: the one-press action per group ("Yes to the 2 the voice backs") has an obvious home. What it loses: the biggest thing is no longer first by right, and on a run that finds one of each kind the page is four headings over four cards.

Either variant keeps the same cards, the same pills, the same yes and no. The pick is only the order on the page.

No name box, no Role, no merge: the card has nothing a person would change. The check and the names never see these lines; the memo says "the call system announced".

In the viewer the Call system's lines keep their place in time, in grey italics with their own label, so the reader knows the words were said and that nobody on the call said them. They are not hidden: an announcement that a minute remains is part of the record.

On the strip it is one more chip, grey, with its count. On the Speakers page its card has no controls, only a sample to hear and the pill "not a person". Found by the shipped phrase list in the first release; the voice step adds, in the second, the lines that sound unlike every party.

On a two-channel call each Side shows one party and the Call system where heard; the Side rule names the party and never the system.

Also called goes to recognition, to the name rules, to Search and to Gideon. Also heard as goes to Search, Find and Gideon and never to recognition. Neither changes a label: every page shows "Dana Reyes".

Two lists, said apart. Also called is what people say ("Mama", "Dana"); Also heard as is what the engine wrote ("Dana Rays"). They look alike but go to different places, and the line under them says which.

The chain is one muted line: how each recording came to carry this name, by hand or by voice and who accepted it. It is what Unlearn will undo, so it is shown before Unlearn is pressed.

Unlearn the voice is the row's danger button, last in the row: it deletes the Person's Voice print and returns every Speaker the voice matched to unnamed and marked. The names given by hand stay.

Grouped by Person, not by recording. The question the tab asks is "is this Dana?", once, with every recording she might be on listed under it with its rating. Yes to the ones rated high is the one-press path; a medium stays one at a time, as the plan's ceiling for a print taught by one recording asks.

A conflict is a card of its own and proposes nothing until a person hears all three. The Side rule's alarm never renames: the case rule said Side 1 is Dana on every call, the voice says not on this one, and the card asks.

Decided so far per source is the measure the plan judges each release by, on the page where the office can read it, and the dismissal alarm is a line under a Person whose matches keep being wrong.
