Moving the circle (added 2026-11-04): drag anywhere inside the circle and the whole circle moves with the pointer, the map staying still under it; the handle on its edge still widens and narrows it. Until now only the small dot at its centre moved it, which nobody could be expected to find. The answer, the lanes' bands and the Make an incident button follow the circle when it is let go.

One button under the circle's answer. With a circle drawn, the list's foot gains Make an incident from the circle: the cameras that passed inside, ticked or not, and the span from the first camera's entry to the last camera's exit. The strip shows that span as a bracket across the lanes before anything is made, so the person sees what the incident would cover.

What it makes. The Incidents tab's own New incident, through the same door the map's forming offer already uses (Figure B), so the incident is made the way every incident is: the cameras become its cameras, synced by their clocks, and the page lands on the new incident with its wall and its chronology.

What changes. Two things: the path file's clock steps above sound, and the file name's minute becomes a placement of last resort for body cameras. Everything a person chose by hand stays as they chose it; the app only moves cameras that it placed itself.

Why the name's minute is last. It is only ever accurate to the minute and assumes the camera named its file in UTC, which held for every one of the office's samples but is still one office's habit. A camera placed that way is up to fifty-nine seconds off its neighbours, which the incident page's wall will show as voices not quite lining up; a sound match then pulls it true, and the person sees the pill change from By its name to By sound.

When it happens. A path file added or paired places its camera then and there; a camera already placed by sound is moved to the file's clock with a row saying so ("Placed on the clock, from its path file, over a sound match"). Nothing is re-read on the upgrade: the office's existing placements stand until a file is added or a camera is paired.

A suggestion is a card, numbered. Under the strip, Suggested incidents lists what the app can see: who, when, for how long, where (the street, when a basemap is installed), why it thinks so (from their paths: within 50 m for over five minutes; from their notes; from sound), and that none of them is in an incident yet. Make an incident opens the New incident box filled, exactly as the circle does (Figure B); On the map opens the Map tab with that suggestion's circle drawn; Not this puts it away, remembered, so it is not offered again.

The same suggestion in three places. Its chip on the strip's Suggested row, its card, and on the map a circle in the incidents' colour round the cameras with a small chip to press. Pressing any one lights the other two. The map's old forming block at the foot of the camera list goes; a circle drawn by hand (Figure A) and a circle the app drew are the same thing to press.

Where each press lands. The Incidents tab has no map of its own: the strip and the cards are what it shows. On the map leaves the Incidents tab for the case's Map tab, opened on that day with the suggestion's circle already drawn and its cameras ticked, the way an incident's row's On the map opens it today. Make an incident opens the New incident box right there on the Incidents tab (no map in it), and when it is made the page lands on the incident's own page, whose Map panel beside Cameras shows those cameras moving on the incident's clock (v1.191.0). So the map is reached from the Incidents tab, not shown inside it (choice 9).

What the app sees. Paths first, now that it has them: cameras within 50 m of one another for over five minutes, on one clock, are a suggestion with a place. Then the notes, as the Incidents tab forms them today. Then sound matches between cameras not on the clock. A suggestion is never made into an incident by the app: the press is the person's.

The three streams in "Not on the clock". With Figure C's order, pairing their path files places them from the unit's own clock, and the panel's row says "Placed from its path file" instead of "No time in its file". For a unit whose file is still waiting, the row gains "its path file is waiting for this video" so the person knows why.

The choices this drawing makes

  1. Make an incident from the circle opens the New incident box filled (drawn); or makes the incident at once with the offered name and lands on it.
  2. The incident takes the cameras that passed inside, with the day's others listed unticked (drawn); or the ticked cameras, whatever the circle says.
  3. The name from the street under the circle with the time (drawn); or the time alone, always.
  4. The path file's clock above sound, moving a sound-placed camera when a file comes (drawn); or above sound only for cameras not yet placed.
  5. The file name's minute places a body camera last, marked By its name (drawn); or the map keeps using it and the chronology does not.
  6. Suggestions as numbered cards under the strip, with a Suggested row of chips on the strip and circles on the map (drawn); or cards alone, the strip's forming row simply removed.
  7. Not this is remembered per suggestion for the case (drawn); or per person, so a colleague still sees it.
  8. Paths first, then notes, then sound as the sources of suggestions (drawn); or notes first, as today, with paths added.
  9. The Incidents tab shows no map: On the map goes to the Map tab with the circle drawn, and a made incident lands on its own page with its Map panel (drawn); or a small map under the suggestion cards on the Incidents tab itself, showing the lit suggestion's circle without leaving the tab.
