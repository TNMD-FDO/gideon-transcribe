The recording page, redrawn

The ask (2026-11-09): redesign the recording page for how it is used, with intuitive navigation between the case's recordings, a Map tab that shows the officer's position moving against the lines being said, and an end to the Resume pop-out covering a line. Intuitive and polished.

What the page is for, as the office uses it
  • A reviewer plays one recording of a case (a body camera, an in-car unit, a jail call) and reads along: the transcript is the work, the picture and the sound prove it.
  • They mark what matters as they go: a clip for a hearing, a note on a line, a speaker's name corrected, a moment described; then the next recording of the same case, often the same minutes from another camera.
  • Where a camera has a path file, where the officer stood when a line was said is evidence too (Phase 12 chapter 4 gave each line a where), and today it sends the reader away to the case's Map tab.
  • The screens: office laptops at 1366 by 647 undocked, ultrawides at 2560 and 3440 docked. Both have to read.
What the page does today that gets in the way (figure 1)
  1. On a laptop the words start 600 px down a 647 px window: the title wraps to two lines and a third for the pencil, then the search row, the timeline, the tab bar, two rows of speaker chips, the "Click a name to change it" tip and the Suggest names line. One line of transcript shows.
  2. The case's other recordings are a list under the video, folded on a laptop; there is no previous and next, no sense of "3 of 8", and the list repeats the file names at full length.
  3. The Resume pill floats over the top of the transcript, centred, and covers whichever line is under it; it is the warning colour for something that is not a warning.
  4. The map is a tab of the case, not of the recording: where on a line answers in words and "On the map" leaves the page.
  5. The head's title is the file's full name, which the case tabs' trail already shows in small; the pencil to rename it takes a line of its own.

Three directions follow, then the pieces they share (the recording walker, the Map tab, the Resume state, the laptop height budget) and the pick. The words are made up; the shape is the app's.

Figure 6. The Map tab, against the lines being said

The recording's Map tab is the case's map (the same script, following this page's clock as it follows the incident page's) with two things the case's tab does not have. The camera's own path is lit and its marker moves with the playhead; the other cameras of the case on the same clock are drawn grey, with their distance from this one. And every line of the transcript is a pip on the path where the camera was when it was said (the where answer, drawn instead of written): a ring, filled for the line being spoken; hover shows the line, a press seeks to it. Beside the map a narrow Said here list keeps the words in view: the line being spoken in the middle, the lines before and after, scrolling with the clock, each pressable; a line said with no fix (indoors, before the file starts) is a dashed ring. The caption under the map is the Now line. Nothing new is asked of the server: the case's map JSON and the path file's fixes the page already has give every position.

Following the clockThe marker moves with the playhead as the recording plays or is dragged; the map pans to keep it in view unless the reader has moved the map, then a "Follow" pill brings it back, as the Map tab's clock does today.
Other camerasThe case's other cameras with a fix on the same clock are drawn grey at their place at this moment, each with its distance; a press on one opens that recording at the same moment, which is also what the walker's › does for the next camera.
Lines saidA pip per line with a fix; a tick on the timeline's foot for every line too (figures 2 and 4), so the timeline reads as "where the talking is" as well as "where the sound is". A line with no fix is on the list with a dashed ring and no pip.
Who was near here, ReadingsThe case Map tab's tools stay on its tab; here the Readings pill lists this camera's own readings (lights, siren, mic muted, camera off) on the path as the case's map draws them, since they are this recording's facts.
When there is no path fileThe tab is not there, as the case's Map tab is not there without one. A jail call never shows it. A camera whose file is waiting shows the tab greyed with "waiting for its path file".
What it costsThe map script already follows a page's clock (the incident page's pane) and already answers where per line; the pips are that answer drawn for every line once, from the fixes the page holds. The basemap is the office's own tiles.

Figure 9. The Map tab by what the recording holds

The maintainer took A plus C and asked for something more alive than a greyed "waiting for its path file", since some recordings will never have one. The tab is decided from three facts the page already knows: the recording's kind (a jail call or a phone line can never carry a camera's file; a body camera or an in-car unit can), whether it is placed on the case's clock, and what files the case holds for this camera and the others. The page keeps watching those facts as it already watches the transcript's state, so a file that lands while the page is open makes the tab appear without a reload.

Added, the card goes and the map fills in place, the camera's path lit and the pips drawn, without leaving the page.
The rule in one lineThe tab is there when there is a map worth drawing for this recording: its own path, the paths around it on the clock, or a file about to become one. It is not there when nothing could be drawn, and the page never says "waiting" for a recording that will never have a file.
Honest about what it showsAn "around" map draws other cameras' positions, never a guess at this one's: no marker, no pips, no distance from this camera, and the caption says why. That keeps a reader from taking a colleague's position for the wearer's.
Nothing new from the serverThe kind, the clock placement, the case's paths and the waiting files are all in the page's state and the case's map JSON already; the tab's four shapes are decided in the browser, and the state poll the page already runs makes them change in place.

Taken (2026-11-09): Direction A, plus C from 2200 wide, with the Map tab's four shapes of figure 9 for recordings with and without a path file.

The pick as first drawn: Direction A as the page on every screen, with Direction C's three columns from 2200 wide (the office's docked screens), and the pieces of figures 5 to 7 in both. The stage-and-work-area shape was chosen on a review two months ago and the reviewers know it; what the laptop needs is not a new shape but its height back (figure 8), and what the ultrawide needs is to spend its width on the case's recordings and on the map beside the words rather than on a wider table. The Map as a tab (figure 6) rather than a stage switch (Direction B) gives the map room to read, keeps the picture playing where it is, and on the wide screen opens beside the Transcript, which is the "against the lines" the ask names; the Said here list keeps the words with the map on a laptop, where a tab must replace the transcript. Direction B is the alternative if the picture and the map should never be apart from the words on a laptop; it fits in the same page as a later addition (the stage switch) if wanted.

How it would be built, if taken: four releases, each walked. (1) The head and the height (the walker, the one-line title with hover, the folded speakers with the ⓘ, the follow state in the tab bar, the timeline's strip as a tooltip): a template and stylesheet release, no server change. (2) The walker's list and keys, with the same-moment hand-off between cameras on the clock. (3) The Map tab: the map script's clock mode for a recording page (as the incident page's), the pips and the Said here list, the timeline's line ticks. (4) The three columns from 2200 wide. The specification gets a chapter in Phase 13 for the four, since chapter 8 of Phase 1 describes the Desk as it stands.
