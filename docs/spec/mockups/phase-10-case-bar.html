The case bar on every page inside a case

Today only the case page draws the case's tabs; the recording page, the incident page, the document page, the Notes page and the Speakers pages each point back a different way, and from some of them the case is two hops away. The drawing puts one bar at the top of every page that belongs to a case: the case's name, then the nine tabs with their counts, the tab the page lives under lit, with the page's own name after it. One press reaches any other part of the case. Made-up case.

Narrow windows scroll the bar sideways instead of wrapping it into three lines; the lit tab is brought into view when the page opens. The case page's own bar keeps wrapping as it does today.

Figure D

How it is built

One include, one helper.
The case page's tab markup moves into an include, case-bar.html, drawn from one helper that gathers the counts in a handful of cheap count queries (recordings, proposals waiting, notes, documents, incidents, clips, people named) and says which tabs the office has on. The case page uses it for its own bar, so there is one bar, not two that drift apart.
Each page says its tab.
A page that belongs to a case hands the include its case, the tab it lives under and its own name: the recording page "recordings", the incident page "incidents", the document page "documents", the Notes page "notes", the Speakers pages "recordings" with the recording's name. The base layout draws the bar above the page's content when a page gives one.
What it costs.
A handful of count queries on the five pages that did not have them; the recording page and the incident page already measure their queries in the suite, so the ceilings move by that handful. No migration, no new setting, no glossary word: the bar is the case page's tabs, reached from more pages.

The case page is untouched. Its name, its line of counts and its tabs stay exactly where they are; the include only draws what it drew. Left: the case page today, the Recordings tab open on the list.

A deeper page is that head carried along. Right: BWC2-1 opened from the list. The case's name sits where it sat, smaller and a link; the tabs sit under it in the same place; the lit tab has "› BWC2-1" after it; and the recording's own head and player sit below as the tab's content, the way the list sat below on the left. The eye reads it as the same page gone one level deeper, not a new window with its own navigation: nothing is added on the right, the rail at the left is the only other navigation, and no page opens in a new window.

What goes, so nothing is doubled. The deeper pages' own ways back to the case (the back arrow and crumb, "in the case X", Back to the case) go, since the head is now the way back. Only the finer links stay, inside the page: Back to the incident on a document, Back to the recording on the Speakers pages. The case's line of counts stays on the case page alone.

Choices

Two things only the maintainer can decide

  1. Counts on the bar away from the case page. Recommended: the counts, as the case page shows them, so the bar reads the same everywhere and says at a glance that proposals are waiting. Or: names alone on the other pages, lighter to read and to query.
  2. What the case's name in the bar opens. Recommended: the case page on its Recordings tab, the case's front door. Or: the tab the person last had open on that case, which the case page already remembers for the Notes tab's shape but not for the tab itself.

What does not change. The pages' own heads, panels, layers and Back links that do a finer job; the rail at the left; the Preview's window; pages opened in a window of their own. The case page's bar is the same bar, drawn in the same place.

Size. One release, no migration: the include and the helper, six templates, the base layout's one slot, the two query ceilings, the spec's Phase 8 chapter 1 ("The tabs, everywhere") amended to say the bar follows the case onto its pages.
