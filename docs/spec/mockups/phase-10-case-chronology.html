Nothing squished. The chronology takes the Notes tab's whole column, where the rows were. The pressed entry plays in the case page's own right pane, where the Preview already lives for citations and search hits; nothing new is pushed in.

One day at a time. The day tabs carry the note counts; the ruler, the band and the spells are the day in hand. A recording that runs past midnight is on both days.

The rail. Recordings not yet on the clock sit above the ruler as chips, each with its note count, until they are placed (figure B). The band is the recordings placed, in their colours, with how each was placed in the legend.

The spells. The incident's drawing: a gap of ten minutes starts a new spell; an entry is the note's words, the line it sits under in the muted colour (or "at a moment, nothing said"), the writer, the recording's chip, and To check when someone has asked. The lit entry is the one playing.

Time unknown. A recording placed by its day alone sits in a band at the end of the day, so a jail call is on the right day without an invented hour. Its entry's time reads the day.

Export in the lead: Chronology to Word (figure C) and as a spreadsheet, the incident's two, for the day in hand or every day.
