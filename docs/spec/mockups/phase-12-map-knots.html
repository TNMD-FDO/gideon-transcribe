Knots of markers, and an easy way to let a moved name go

Two asks of 2026-11-04 after v1.195.1, from the office's own map: a name dragged somewhere stays locked, which is right, but a person needs an easy way to put it back; and when two or three cameras stand on one spot the markers, their short unit letters, a vehicle's speed tag and the name tags pile up on each other. The fictional office throughout; the picture the office sent is redrawn here with made-up names.

Figure A

Today: what piles up on a knot

Four things fight for the same twenty pixels. The marker heads with their unit letters cut to "BW"; a vehicle's dark speed tag, which sits on whatever is beside the marker; the name tags, laid out clear of each other but allowed to touch the markers; and the markers themselves, which stand where the cameras stood and may not be moved. The same officer's body camera and vehicle also show the name twice.

Figure B

The same knot after: dots, the speed inside the tag, room round every marker

Markers become dots. A marker is its colour and its shape (round for a body camera, square for a vehicle), 14 px, with no letters inside: the name tag beside it says who, and the unit is on hover and in the row. The lights and siren badges stay, as does the hollow marker for a camera that has ended and the dimmed one for no fix. The letters come back only where there is no tag to say the name, which is nowhere now.

The speed moves into the vehicle's tag. The dark speed chip is kept, but it sits inside the vehicle's name tag at its end ("Ofc. Marsh vehicle [6 mph]", changing as it drives; a parked vehicle shows "0 mph"), and the loose tag beside the marker goes. One fewer thing on the map, the number as readable as before, and sitting with the name it belongs to. The lane head and the tile's bar keep showing it too.

Room round every marker. A name tag, a tile and a note pip (the earlier fix) are kept 12 px clear of every marker drawn, not only their own, and the pips get a wider hit ring. Markers themselves stay exactly where the cameras stood: two cameras on one spot are two dots a few pixels apart, the pressed one drawn on top; zooming in parts them, and the names were never on top of them.

One officer, two cameras. The vehicle's tag keeps "vehicle" after the name so the two read apart; the drawing does not merge them (choice 4).

Figure C

Letting a moved name go

  • Today a pinned name carries an 8 px blue dot that releases it, which nobody can be expected to find, and Tidy names sits among nine other buttons on the transport.
  • The release reads as words: hovering a pinned name grows a small "pinned · release" chip at its end; a press on it, or a double press anywhere on the name, lets it ride again. On touch, a long press shows the chip.
  • Tidy names stays for the lot, and the transport also says how many are pinned ("3 pinned") so a person knows there is something to tidy.
  • A tile's pin button in its bar already does this for tiles; it gets the same hover words.
Figure D

A tag that stands for more than one video: a press asks which

  • With two tags per officer (choice 4 as drawn) a body camera's tag is one video and opens its tile at once. Nothing to choose.
  • A vehicle's tag already stands for its two or three streams, which the map draws as one marker; today a press opens the first stream with no way to the others. The press now shows a small chooser under the tag, Front · Cabin · Rear (the streams as the files name them, else "stream 1, 2, 3"), and the tile opens on the one pressed. The tile's bar carries the same chooser to switch.
  • If you preferred one merged tag for an officer's body camera and vehicle, the same chooser would serve it: "Body camera · Vehicle front · Vehicle cabin". The drawing keeps two tags, since the vehicle has its own speed and its own line to its own marker.
  • The chooser is a press away, not a hover, so it works by touch; Escape or a press elsewhere closes it.
For your word

The choices this drawing makes

  1. Markers become plain dots with no letters, 14 px (drawn); or keep the letters but only when the map is zoomed in close enough for them to fit.
  2. The speed goes into the vehicle's name tag and the dark speed tag goes (drawn); or keep both.
  3. Names, tiles and note pips keep 12 px clear of every marker (drawn); or 8 px, tighter.
  4. An officer's body camera and vehicle keep two tags, "vehicle" telling them apart (drawn); or one tag with two dots when both stand in the same knot.
  5. Release by the hover chip or a double press, and "N pinned" on the transport beside Tidy names (drawn); or the chip alone.
  6. A vehicle's tag asks which stream with a small chooser under it, and the tile's bar switches streams (drawn); or the tag opens the front stream and the row's ▸ lists the others.
