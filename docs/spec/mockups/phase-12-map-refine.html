Phase 12, chapter 6 · a drawing for the maintainer's word · 2026-11-04

The Map tab, refined: pick the cameras you want, names first, a video beside each marker, and path files that wait for their video

The asks of 2026-11-04: show only the cameras a person ticks (one, several or all); let the trails go off entirely; make the list beside the map minimal, the officer's name first and matching the lanes under, the rest behind a disclosure; when many cameras stand in one spot, lead a line from each marker out to its name, and let a press on the name open a small video that moves with the camera, draggable, four or more at once, with a vehicle's speed shown; keep path files uploaded before their videos (two hundred or more at a time) from clogging the chat or anything else; a circle a person can widen on the map to find the cameras that passed inside it; and move Add path files to the case's head, left of Add recordings. Added the same day: a video dragged on the map stays where it is dropped; a note on one recording at the moment its video is on; and a note that applies to all the recordings at that moment. The fictional office of the local demo throughout; no real place or name is drawn.

Ticks. Every camera of the day has a tick box; the map, the strip and the chat's "following" see only the ticked ones. The header's box ticks all or none (a half mark when some are ticked); a press on a name alone ticks only that camera (the way a click on one row in a list does), Shift adds. The choice is remembered per person per case. Unticked rows stay in the list, dimmed, so nothing is lost.

Names first. The row is the colour dot and the name the path file gives its wearer; a vehicle is its driver's name with "vehicle" after it, a square dot, and its speed while it moves. The lane heads under say the same names in the same order, so the two read as one. A file with no name falls back to the unit (BWC3-1) as now. The unit, the kind, the surety pill, the span and the actions have gone behind the ▸ (Figure B).

Trails off. The Show control reads Markers only, Trails, Whole paths, and "faint paths under" is a tick of its own, off here: nothing is drawn but the markers. Today Markers only still leaves the faint whole path under each marker; that becomes the tick's business. Both are remembered per person.

The crowd and the speed. When two or more markers would overlap, each gets a short line out to its name on a small tag, fanned so the tags do not collide (Figure C has five). A vehicle moving over 3 mph carries its speed on a dark tag beside its marker, in the file's unit.

The head's door. Add path files now stands in the case's head to the left of Add recordings, on every tab, and leaves the Map tab's transport. A path file is the case's input, not the map's: it feeds the chronology, the chat, the memo and the Speakers page as well. Fit all became Fit shown.

What the ▸ holds

  • The facts: the unit as the file gives it, the kind, the surety pill with its why on hover, the span on the clock, a vehicle's streams.
  • Where it is now: the street under the marker when a basemap is installed, and the nearest other camera with its distance (the readings' Who was near, read at the clock's moment). Never a coordinate.
  • The actions: Open a tile (Figure C), Play here (the Preview as today), Fly to, Readings (the list from release 3), Replace file, Remove. Add a path file sits on a row without one.
  • One row opens at a time; opening another closes the first. Hovering a row still lights its path and marker; the opened row says where the camera is, updating with the clock.

The vehicle's row shows its streams as pills in the open part; its marker and lane are still one (the twin rule of release 1). Its speed sits at the row's end whether open or not, where the ▸ would be.

The leaders. Five markers stand within a few metres at the corner. Each gets a thin line from its marker out to a tag with its colour and name; the tags are placed round the knot so none covers another or a marker, and they move with the clock. Pressing a tag (or the name in the list) opens that camera's tile; a tag whose tile is open has a blue edge. Leaders appear only when markers would overlap at the map's zoom; a lone marker keeps its small label as today.

Tiles. A tile is a small window playing one camera's video at the clock's moment, muted; its bar is the camera's colour, name and, for a vehicle, its speed. Up to six at once (four drawn). A tile opens beside its marker and rides with it as the camera moves, tethered by a dashed line; drag it anywhere and it pins there (the pin turns blue) and stays put through pans, zooms and the clock's running, until it is closed or dragged again. The tether is redrawn every tick from the tile to wherever the officer's marker is now, so the line always points at the officer as they move; when the marker leaves the view the line runs to the pane's edge in their direction, and the tile's bar reads "out of view". One tile at a time may have its sound on (the speaker icon). The clock drives every tile; Pause pauses them all; "close all" on the transport.

Notes from a tile. The ✎ in a tile's bar writes a note on that recording at the moment its video is on, through the recording's own note-at-a-moment door (the one the Preview and the event cards already use), marked as coming from the map, so the Notes tab's hover and the exports say so. Note on all shown, on the transport, writes one note on every ticked camera at the clock's moment of each: five copies carrying one shared mark, so the Notes tab lists it once as "on 5 cameras", the chronology shows one entry, and editing or removing it from any copy asks whether to do the same on all. Nothing new is kept; each copy is an ordinary moment note with the usual audit row.

Speeds. Playing at 1× every tile plays video; at 4× they play silently at four times; at 16× and 60× a browser cannot play that fast, so each tile shows the frame at the clock's moment, refreshed each second, and says so in its bar. Six videos decoding at once is the heaviest thing the page does; the tile's picture is small, and the chapter sets the cap at six for that reason.

What a tile is not. It is not the Preview (the floating window with its caption and note box, still Play here): no caption, no note, no lead-in, in step with the clock, several at once. The glossary gets a word for it (choice 3).

Drawing the circle. Press Within a circle, then press a place on the map: a circle of 50 m appears there with a handle on its edge. Drag the handle to widen or narrow it (the radius reads beside it, in metres as the readings use), drag the centre to move it. Only one circle at a time; "clear the circle" or Escape removes it. It is Who was near here (release 3) grown a handle: the same question, a place and a distance, answered from the fixes in the browser.

The answer. The list regroups: the cameras whose path crossed the circle at any time of the day, each with the span it was inside (its first entry to its last exit), the rest under "Never inside". Each lane marks the inside spells in a faint blue band, so a person sees at once who was there together and when. Tick these ticks only the cameras that passed inside and clears the rest; a press on a row's span seeks the clock to its first entry.

What it is not. Not a saved thing: the circle lives on the page and goes with it. Nothing about it is kept, written to an audit row or sent to the chat; a circle worth keeping is a note or an incident, both a press away as before. The circle's centre is a position and so never leaves the browser.

Later, when the video lands in the case

BWC7-2 added · its waiting path file was paired: 812 fixes

The Details tab's About this case counts them too: "5 cameras on the map, 2 without a path file, 201 path files waiting for their video."

Kept, not read. A path file that matches no video in the case is no longer a leftover to settle by hand; it is kept on the case as a waiting file: its bytes, its name, its kind and cue count from a first look, who added it and when. It has no Path yet, so it is on no map, in no lane, in no chat block, no memo, no comparison, no Speakers page offer, and no readings. Only the fold on the Map tab and the Details count know it exists.

Pairing. When a recording arrives in the case (uploaded, moved in, or named again) the app looks for a waiting file with its name, by the same stem rule the check list uses, and pairs it: the file becomes the recording's Path with the usual "Path file added" audit row, and a toast says so. The unit and length checks still apply; a disagreement is flagged in the recording's row rather than paired blindly. A waiting file whose video never comes stays until someone removes it or the case is deleted.

The one design change. Release 1 settled that a Path belongs to its Recording (ADR 0029) and that an unmatched file is answered then and there. Waiting files need a small table of their own on the case (a migration) and an amendment to that ADR: the file may wait on the case; the Path is still made only on a Recording. Nothing waiting is ever parsed into readings, so nothing erroneous reaches the chat.

From the cameras' readings. The 201 path files waiting for their videos were not read.

  • The chat reads only paths. The readings block names a camera by its wearer first ("Ofc. Amari (BWC2-1)"), as the list now does. Waiting files are not in it, and the answer's foot says how many were left unread, so nobody wonders.
  • Ticks do not reach the chat. What a person ticks is a view on the page, not the case's truth: the chat, the memo and the comparison keep reading every camera with a Path. A question about "the cameras shown" is a view the page could pass later if wanted; not drawn here.
  • Many cameras. A case with dozens of paths already trims its block to the token budget, longest-standing readings first; the waiting files keep that block from growing with every file uploaded ahead of its video.
For your word

The choices this drawing makes, to keep or change

  1. Ticks remembered per person per case (drawn), or fresh each visit with everyone ticked.
  2. The name shown is the wearer as the path file gives it, falling back to the unit; a vehicle is its driver's name with "vehicle" (drawn). Or the unit first with the name small, as today.
  3. The word for the small video window: Tile (drawn; avoid pop-out, mini-player, thumbnail, picture-in-picture). Or another word of yours.
  4. How many tiles at once: six at most (four drawn), one with sound; or four.
  5. A new tile rides with its marker until dragged, then pins and stays where dropped, its line following the officer's marker as they move (your words of 2026-11-04; drawn); the pinned place is kept for that camera while the tab is open, not across visits.
  6. Leaders appear only when markers overlap at the current zoom (drawn), or always, on every marker.
  7. The speed tag beside a moving vehicle's marker (drawn) is on whenever it moves over 3 mph; or only while the vehicle is ticked and the clock plays.
  8. Trails off is "Markers only" plus the "faint paths under" tick off (drawn); or a fourth Show choice "Nothing".
  9. The circle starts at 50 m and reads in metres (drawn), or in feet with the scale bar.
  10. Add path files moves to the head and leaves the transport (drawn); or stays in both places.
  11. Waiting files are kept raw on the case and parsed only when paired (drawn), so they cost nothing to hold; or parsed on arrival so the fold can show their kind and cue count (a first look, as drawn, reads only the header and counts cues).
  12. Pairing on arrival covers upload, move-in and rename (drawn); the flagged cases wait in the recording's row rather than pair.
  13. A note on all shown is one moment note per ticked camera with a shared mark, shown once on the Notes tab (drawn); or a new kind of note kept on the case at a time of day, which would need a table of its own and a place on the Notes tab. The drawn way reuses what exists and lands the note on each recording's own page too.
One thing to flag before building. Waiting files change what release 1 decided: a path file that matched no video was answered at once with a chooser, and a Path has always belonged to a Recording. The drawing keeps the second rule (a Path is made only on a Recording) and bends the first (the file may wait on the case). That needs a small new table on the case, a migration, and an amendment to ADR 0029. The rest of the drawing is page work on what exists.
