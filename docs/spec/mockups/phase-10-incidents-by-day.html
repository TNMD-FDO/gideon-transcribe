The same day, suggested. Videos on one day that are in no incident and in no forming bracket get a dotted same day bracket on the strip and a day card below: who they are, a still of each at its start, how they stand to the day's incidents and forming ones in time, and three presses: Add to the forming one (or to the day's incident, when there is one and no forming one), Choose myself (New incident with them ticked), and Its own matter, which puts the suggestion away for those videos by the offers' key. The day card never says "incident" of them; it says they share a day.

A still to choose by. Every card and every picker row carries a still: one frame of each video at the same moment, the span's start, cut from the playback copy when the card is drawn (the frame grabber the notes export already uses, a few tenths of a second a frame) and never kept, as Phase 4 rules. The forming card's three stills show the same moment from three cameras, which is the quickest way to see that they belong together, or that one of them is a different stop.

The surety on every bar. The bars' labels carry the word v1.183.0 put beside the placed pill, so the strip says which of the day's videos stand on a checked clock and which on a typed time.

Not on the clock, one place. The rail's chips become a panel under the cards: every video of the case with no clock, with or without notes, one row each with its still, what its file says, and what its sound says once the second release runs the match for it against the placed videos of its file's day (or of every day, when the file carries no time, as Unclear). Place it there, Type a time, Place by the day, Not this. The day chips count them. Audio recordings with notes keep the rail on the Notes tab's chronology, where their notes are; this panel is the videos', since incidents are videos.

Cost. A picker of twenty rows is twenty frames, cut in parallel as the browser asks for them, about a second on the office's server; a still is served as content, private and never stored, by a small address of its own that only a person who may open the recording can read.

The strip keeps one height, by the chronology's own rules. Over six videos overlapping, the band is the Coverage lane: one shaded strip whose height is how many were running at each moment, with a press at any moment for the list of those running then; under it, bars for the eight most noted only, and "show all 32" opens a capped band of eight rows that scrolls inside itself. Thirty-two bars are never stacked. The notes lane merges marks that would overlap at the scale, with a count. The forming lane draws every bracket but labels only those with room; a bracket too narrow for its words shows them on hover.

Three cards, always. The forming brackets with the most notes first, then the day card; the rest are "and N more on the strip", reached by pressing a bracket. A card shows at most six stills and says how many more wait in the picker. The sitting bar on a card near the ceiling says so in the warning colour, since eleven cameras is where one reading of the engine runs out.

The panel shows five, not nine. Ordered by what the app can say: a Clear sound line first, then Likely, then a file time, then nothing; "show all" opens the rest. A row never holds more than one still and two lines.

The picker at thirty-two. As the chapter has it: the eight most noted ticked and shown, "and 24 more ran in this span with no notes · Choose more" opening the rest in a list that scrolls inside the sheet, each with its still; the count reads against Most cameras in an incident ("11 of 20 cameras · 9 hours' worth") and the sitting bar redraws on each tick. Thirty-two stills are cut only when the person asks for the rest, eight at a time as they scroll.

The Date strip. One bar per day whatever the day holds; the dot and the ring are the only marks. A case with ninety days is ninety narrow bars, as today.

For your word

What it would take, in order

Recommended: three releases, Figure A to C as drawn. First the same-day suggestion and the day marks (the strip's day card and dotted bracket, the chips' counts, the Date strip's dots; no new store beyond the offers' keys). Then the stills (one small address that cuts a frame on request from the playback copy, used by the cards, the panel and the picker; nothing stored). Then the panel for what is not on the clock with placing by sound (the strip's second release as drawn on 2026-10-30, now with the surety words: the match against the placed videos of the file's day first, every day as Unclear when the file carries no time; a new way of placing in the recording's clock, by sound, with its surety; one migration).

  • Words. "Same day" for the dotted bracket and its card (never "incident" until made); "Not on the clock" for the panel, the rail's words already; "placed by sound" joins the Recording's clock's ways, with its Surety.
  • Rules to confirm. A same-day card needs two or more loose videos on the day, or one loose video on a day that has an incident or a forming one. Its own matter puts the set away by the offers' key. A still is cut at the span's start for a forming card and at each video's own start for a day card and the panel; it is never kept and never exported from here. The panel lists videos only; audio with notes stays on the rail.
  • Cost. Stills: a few tenths of a second a frame on the media worker's ffmpeg, served from the web tier, at most twenty at once. Sound: one match per placed video of the day for each clockless video, a few seconds each on the media worker, when a video lands and when a video is placed.
  • Settings. None new: Incidents proposed governs the day cards as it governs the forming ones; Match by sound governs the panel's sound line.
