Gideon Transcribe, a mock-up

The clock verdict and the Preview in the page: every play button tells the truth about where the sound is, and the Preview is part of the page it serves

Drawn 2026-10-22 after the maintainer found, on one of the office's calls, that a block of lines read 2:05 on the page but was spoken at about 2:21. Measured against the audio's own energy, the block sat sixteen seconds early: the transcript had a hole where the audio had speech, then the block, then another hole. The words and the speaker proposals were right; the clock was not. The same shape appeared nine times across the call. Every name, call and line here is made up.

Two decisions this drawing proposes. First, the clock verdict is a fact of the Transcript, written as it lands for every Recording of every type, and read by every place that plays a moment: the transcript, the samples, the line screen, the "hear" buttons, the Preview, the chat's citations and the search's hits. Second, the Preview stops being a card that pops up at the bottom right and becomes a panel of the page it opened from, one design for all the places it opens.

What does not move. No word changes and no line moves. The Speakers page stays the one place a proposal is accepted or declined. The recogniser's stack stays pinned; the verdict is made from the finished transcript and the audio, not by changing how the words are recognised.

Rule 1. A hole the audio fills. Eight seconds or more with no line, where the audio has sound for most of it (over seven tenths). The lines on either side of the hole are the suspects.
Rule 2. A line over a pause. A line of two seconds or more whose words are said to run across a pause the audio shows (over a second of quiet inside it). Words are not spoken through silence.
Rule 3. The bound. A block that fails rule 2 and sits beside a hole from rule 1 is marked "the clock here may be off by up to N s", N being the hole's length, never a guess beyond it. The block is not moved; the mark says where to listen.
When it runs. As every transcript lands, after the Call system's pass and before the Speaker check, on every type of recording, and again on Process again. Older transcripts get it once, the way the Call system's pass was given later. The audio's pauses are measured once and kept with the transcript as facts about the processing, never as content.

Why these two rules and not the recogniser's own confidence. The recogniser cuts the audio by voice activity, writes words per chunk, and the aligner places each word inside its chunk's window. When it writes words for only part of a chunk, the words land at the window's start and a hole appears after them where the speech really is. The recogniser reports no doubt about this; only the audio does. Measured on the office's call: nine sound-filled holes and twenty-nine lines over a pause, out of a hundred and ninety-nine longer lines.

What a mark changes elsewhere. A marked line plays a wider window and says so (figures 2 and 3), gives the voice step no vector and so teaches no print and rates no match (figure 6), is never a Sample, and a Preview that would seek to it seeks to the sound instead (figure 4). The "timing is a guess" mark of v1.141.0 stays for thin and repeated lines; this is the second kind of doubt, about a whole block.

Where it opens. The panel takes the context column while a person's voice is being heard, so this voice's lines on the left and her voice on the right are side by side; Close, Escape or picking a name gives the column back. On the recording page and the case page it takes the page's own right column the same way (figure 5). It never floats over anything.

What it carries, every time. A heading with the person, their role, the recording and the moment, and the clock verdict as a pill; the person's samples from that recording as a row of plays; the picture where there is one; the few lines around the moment with the one being heard lit; a tab per recording that taught the print; Open the recording; its own small player. The note box and All cameras stay where the chat and the Notes page need them (figure 5).

The clock verdict reaches it. The moment the Preview seeks to is a line of the other recording. When that line is marked, the pill reads "clock unsure · heard about 1:04", the player's window widens to where the sound is, drawn as the dashed span on its bar, and the samples offered are only lines whose clock is sure. The picker's play buttons and the "hear" buttons on a Sounds like read the same verdict, so no place in the app plays a confident wrong moment.

The voice step and the marks
No vector from a marked line. The voice step measures a line's voice on the audio between its clock and its end. On a marked line that audio is somebody else's or nobody's, so the line gets no vector, no "agrees" or "against", and it is left out of the Speaker's print on the recording.
No sample from a marked line. The three Samples on a card, the big play of the guided page and the samples the Preview offers come only from lines whose clock is sure. A card says, quietly, "3 lines with an unsure clock, left out".
No print taught from a marked line. When a Speaker is named, its print on the recording is the mean of its sure lines; the marked ones teach nothing, so a wrong clock never becomes a wrong voice remembered across the case or in the unit's book.
The words are not touched. The Speaker check reads the words as before; the proposals stand and are accepted or declined where they always were. On the receipt: "6 lines had an unsure clock; their words were read, their voice was not".

On a phone the Preview is a sheet that slides up from the foot over the lower half of the page, with a handle to pull it taller or away; the page behind stays where it was. The same heading, samples, lines and player as the panel, one column.

Before the build

The one number, measured on the office's recordings first

What is fixed by rule. A hole is eight seconds or more; a pause inside a line is over a second; a hole counts when sound fills over seven tenths of it. These come from the shape measured on the office's call, where the holes were sixteen and twenty-one seconds and the pauses inside lines over a second.
What is measured before it decides anything. On the office's jail calls and body-camera recordings, by ear on a local sheet: how many blocks the two rules mark, how many of those the ear agrees are early, and how many early blocks the rules miss. The thresholds move only if that sheet says so; the sheet never leaves the building.
What is left for later, named. Sliding a marked block into the hole beside it, by its own length, with its words re-aligned there. That changes the transcript's clock, so it is an ADR with the measurement behind it, after the marks have been lived with.
