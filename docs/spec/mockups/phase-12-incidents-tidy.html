The Incidents tab and the incident page tidied: one walk, press by press

Five asks after the builder went on the server: the strip's ruler should span only the time needed; the Reader grows with the ticks and pushes the map down; "Clear" still sits on the bars; the person should be able to set a span by hand and see the cameras that ran in it; and on the incident page the notes do not blend in. One walk through both pages, each figure the page after one press, with the press named in the amber line above it. Drawn for the office's laptops at 1366 wide and for the large ultrawide monitors they dock to; there is no phone or tablet shape to keep. The fictional office throughout.

Figure A

Today, for the record: what the three faults look like at 1366 wide

Step 1

The tab opens: the ruler fits the cameras, the map sits under the strip, the Reader reads the day

Nothing pressed yet. Six cameras on the clock; one ran at lunchtime, the rest at the evening stop.

The ruler. From the first camera's start to the last camera's end, with the five empty hours between the lunchtime camera and the stop folded to a short break that says "5 h". A press on the break opens it for the day. Every bar wide enough carries its span; the surety words are gone.

The span box sits in the lead: two time fields, Set, and "or drag across the ruler". The thin track under the ruler is the drag.

The page. The map is under the strip, in view; the Reader has the right column to itself and reads the day.

Step 2

Set a span by hand: the ruler zooms to it, the cameras that ran in it come to the top, the Reader says who they are

The press: drag across the ruler's track from 20:25 to 20:40, or type 20:25 and 20:40 in the span box and press Set. The same result either way.

What the span does. The ruler zooms to the span with a little either side; the lanes sort by how long each camera ran inside it, the minutes on the head, and a camera that never did sinks and fades; the dashed column with its handles is the span, which the handles and the day line's window move. Nothing is ticked yet, so the map draws nothing.

The Reader becomes Your span. The cameras that ran in it, each with its minutes and its own tick, with Tick all 5 (or, over the cap, Tick the longest 8), the one outside named, and Clear the span. The ticks here and on the lanes are the same ticks.

Three ways to set it. Type the two times and Set; drag across the ruler's track; or, once zoomed, drag the window on the day line. Clear the span returns to the fitted ruler.

Step 3

Tick: the Reader becomes Your incident, the map draws the ticked, the span holds

The press: Tick all 5 in the Reader, or the ticks one by one on the lanes. Then untick Ofc. Dunn, who is already in an incident.

The span holds. A span you set stays the incident's span; ticks do not widen it, and the handles and the window move it. Without a set span the column is the ticked cameras' first start to last end, as it is now, narrowed by the handles.

The map draws the ticked, in view under the strip; the clock line sits at the span's start and the pictures on the lanes and in the Reader are cut there.

The Reader is Your incident, the box you know, in its own column, scrolling inside itself when it grows: the name, the camera lines, who in your span is not ticked, the tools, Make the incident.

Step 4

Look before deciding: drag the clock line, the map and the pictures follow

The press: drag the clock line from 20:25:00 to 20:31:40. The markers on the map move to where each camera was then, and the pictures are cut again when you let go.

Step 5

Make the incident: it opens on its own page at the span's start, and the tab is clean again

The press: Make the incident. The incident is made in your name with the three cameras and the span 20:25 to 20:40; its page opens at 20:25:00. Back on the tab, the span and the ticks are gone and the ruler is fitted again, with the new incident as a shaded column.

What is recorded. As today: who built it, when, from the strip, with the span; the Details tab says "Span set: while building, 6 Nov 2025, 20:25 to 20:40".

What is not. The span, the ticks and the clock line are the tab's for its life; nothing of them is kept when nothing is made.

Then the incident page

The incident made in step 5 opens at 20:25:00, and its Chronology carries the notes the cameras already hold

Where we are: the incident "Harbour Street stop, 20:25" with three cameras, opened at the span's start. The work panel beside the wall shows the Chronology; the notes written on these cameras, on the Notes tab, from the Map tab or in the Preview, are its events. Steps 6 to 9 are how those notes read today and as drawn.

Step 6

On the incident page, today: the Chronology's rows, a pill per note, the same note three times, the marks all alike

Step 7

Drawn: a note is a row like any other, with the camera's colour, a small mark, and the writer in a quiet line that never cuts

One row shape for every event. The time, the camera's colour dot, the words at one size, and a source line under them in the muted small text: who wrote it, which camera, where it came from. A note's row adds the small note mark before its words and nothing else. The pill goes; the line wraps and is never cut.

A note on several cameras is one row. Note on all shown already marks its copies as one for the Notes tab; the Chronology lists them once too, at the earliest moment, with every camera's dot and "on 3 cameras from the map · each at its second". Edit or remove it once and every copy follows, as the Notes tab does.

The other sources read the same way. "Ofc. Pell's words · added by Sam Okafor", "Unit 9082's own file", "accepted from a proposed event", "found by the watch phrase gun". The pill's colour coding is carried by the words.

Step 8

Drawn: the strip's Events lane in the cameras' colours, a note's mark shaped as a note, and the card

The Events lane. A mark takes its camera's colour, as the lanes above it do, and a note's mark is a small flag rather than a tick, so a glance tells a person's note from a camera's reading. Hover says the words; a press opens the card.

The card. The same card every event has, headed "Note at hh:mm:ss on ", the words, the source line, and the cameras it is seen on; Edit and Remove act on the note itself.

Step 9

Drawn: the said band and a tile's line, the writer named

Step 10

Where an event lands on a recording: the time into the video on hover and on the card, and a way there

The press: hover a row, or open its card. Every event already has a camera and a moment on the incident's clock; the page now also says how far into that camera's recording the moment falls, and opens the recording there.

On the row. The source line keeps to who and which camera; a hover on the time gives the long form, "2 min 14 s into Ofc. Amari's video", with the video's name and span, and a shared note's hover lists each camera's.

On the card. The head reads "Note at 20:27:14 · 2 min 14 s into Ofc. Amari's video", and Open the recording opens that page at that second with the note lit; a shared note offers one per camera.

On the strip. The mark is drawn on its camera's lane at the moment as well as on the Events lane, so a glance from a lane finds the note; hover says the camera's own time.

Step 11

A video with no clock and no path file: ticked in anyway, synced on the incident page, its notes waiting until then

The press: tick Cell-9, a phone video with no clock in its picture and no path file, in the Not on the clock row at the strip's foot. It has no lane and no place on the ruler, but you know it belongs to the stop.

Ticked in anyway. The Not on the clock row gives each clockless video a tick. Ticked, it is a camera line in Your incident with a still at its start and the words "not on the clock yet; synced on the incident page by sound or by hand · not on the map", counted against the cap, with no bar on the strip and nothing on the map. The span and the tools pass it by; Your span names it under "not on the clock".

By sound against the ticked. Once two or more cameras are ticked, Put on the clock offers "Match its sound against the cameras you ticked" first, then Type a time and Place by the day as today. The incident takes it unplaced if nothing is chosen, and the incident page's Sync has it.

Its notes wait, in view. On the incident page a note on a camera not yet synced cannot sit among the events, since its moment on the day's clock is unknown; a foot line under the chronology says "Waiting for Cell-9 to be synced · 2 notes" with Sync it, and the notes join at their moments the instant the camera is placed. A note's time into its own video is always known.

Where the incidents built are

Three doors to an incident already built, open at any moment of the build

The press: "2 incidents built" in the lead, which is a button; or the shaded column's name on the strip; or Open in the Reader's day reading. Each lands on the incident's own page.

The lead's button is the door that is always there. "2 incidents built" opens a list of every incident in the case, newest first, with a still, its day and span, who built it and how, Open and On map; it works mid-build, when the Reader is busy being Your incident. Today the pill is a count and nothing more.

The strip and the Reader keep their doors. The shaded column's name reads the incident in the Reader without losing the ticks, and ‹ Your incident goes back; the Reader's day reading lists the day's incidents as it does now.

One more press while building. On an incident's reading the Reader offers Add the ticked cameras to it, so a person who finds the stop already built does not make a second one; the cap and the sitting apply as on Add cameras today.

The whole tab

Put together: the Incidents tab at 1366 wide, mid-build, after every change above

Where we are: the span 20:25 to 20:40 set by hand, three cameras ticked, the clock line dragged to 20:28:10, two more cameras ringed as near. One screen, no scrolling but the Reader's own.

Read top to bottom. The lead says what is on the clock and what you are building; the strip is the day with your span as the bright column and the clock line across it; the Not on the clock row and the foot's one line close the strip; the map under it shows the three ticked paths where they were at 20:28:10; the Reader beside is Your incident, scrolling in its own box, with the tools and Make the incident at the end.

What a non-technical reader meets. Plain words only: cameras, span, your incident, the clock in its picture, not on the clock, pictures at a time. One row shape on the lanes, one in the Reader. Nothing is cut mid-word; the long explanations sit behind the two help marks.

What stays as it is. The case head, the tabs, the day chips, the map's own drawing, Make the incident's door and the incident page it lands on.
