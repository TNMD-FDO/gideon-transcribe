Review as reading: the Speakers page after one run

Chosen on 2026-10-19; Phase 5 chapter 3's "The Speakers page" is written to it. The same page on a phone and the viewer's Speakers strip while changes wait are drawn in phase-5-phone-and-strip.html (chosen 2026-10-20), which also gives each line of a run its own Accept beside Leave this one.

The run's output drawn as a proposed version of the transcript, read once from top to bottom, every judgement with its sound and its lines under the eye. Nothing on the page is a question that needs the person to go elsewhere. Every name and line is made up; the colours are the Speakers' own.

The cast first, with sound. One card per voice: Play its sample, see its colour, read what the run thinks of it. A proposed name sits on the card with the quote that gave rise to it and Play on the quote; the hand-over sits on Speaker 1's card with Play before and after. Nothing to look up.

The lanes show the whole call at a glance, as the page does today. The hand-over is a red marker on Speaker 1's lane at 14:03, with the stretch after it shown in the colour the new Speaker would get. Lines the run would move are shown faintly in their proposed lane, so you see the shape of the change before you read a word.

The transcript is the spine. You read down once. An ordinary line is plain. A proposed change keeps its line in place with the old label struck through and the new one beside it in that Speaker's colour, and Accept or Leave on the right. A run of lines moving together is one block with one Accept all and a quiet Leave this one on each line. Changes only collapses the quiet stretches for a quick pass; Whole transcript shows every line with the same marks for someone reading the call through.

What you are agreeing to is on the line. Each marked line carries three sounds: the line itself, then the two voices as a whole, numbered and coloured, the one it has and the one proposed. Hear the line, hear 1, hear 2, press. The samples are each Speaker's surest lines on this recording, the same ones the cards at the top play.

One button at the top, and names one at a time. The changes the voice backs are taken in a press; names never are, since a wrong name cascades through a Case and its print (the plan rules out Accept all for names and matches). Each name is accepted on its card with its sample and its evidence. The line under the button says the order and that either order is safe, because a name is pinned to the voice that gave rise to it and not to the label. The voice's word is a colour, not a sentence. Backed by the voice: the accent tint, and the first big button takes all of these in a press. The voice against: a red tint and "listen first", never in the one-press path. Unsure: folded, with a button to show them in place for the person who wants to go that far. Unchanged stretches collapse to "no changes" so a 600-line call reads as its 14 marks.

Accepted changes stay where they are, tinted and marked accepted, with Undo beside them, so the reading never jumps. Decided so far counts at the foot. The Call system's lines are in the spine in grey, where they were said, and never marked for anything.

Same idea, one level up. The Person is the spine here: their sample at the top, then one row per recording the voice found them on, each with Play, the rating as a pill, and It is Dana or Not her. The medium one is a plain button; the high ones get the green button and the one-press line at the foot.

The Side rule's alarm is a row like the others, red: the case rule named Side 1 as Dana on Call 7 and the voice says it does not sound like her. Nothing is renamed; the row asks, with Play.

Unlearn the voice stands last in the foot, red text, and says before it acts which recordings it will return to unnamed. The two lists (Also called, Also heard as) open from the row's quiet button rather than crowding it.
