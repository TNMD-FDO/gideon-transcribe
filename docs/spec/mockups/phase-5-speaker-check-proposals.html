The speaker cards ask "is this label really that speaker?" and answer with Merge. The stretch cards ask "do these lines belong to that speaker?" and answer with Accept. Both are right, but they sit in one pile with no line between them, so the page reads as 44 things to approve in two vocabularies, and the one move that would settle most of it (hear two cards, merge) is not said anywhere.

There is also no sense of progress: 179 found, and nothing on the page counts down as you decide.

And each card explains too much. The paragraph under the title does arithmetic across five speakers and then reasons about what it might mean. A person who is not a programmer reads that and stops. The card needs one short line they can nod at.

And above it all, the sketch. Since v1.100.0 the check reads the whole call once and writes itself a sketch of who is who before judging the lines. The page shows that sketch under a fold, "Who is who, as the check read it": today's is 470 words of the engine's own prose. It was put there so a person could see what the check had in mind, but it is model output, not a finding, and on this page it reads as a long explanation nobody asked for. The sketch's wording in the figure is invented, not today's.

Everything is a proposal. One shape of card, one kind of answer: play the moments, say yes or no. The state line says so in one sentence and that is the whole explanation on the page. Each card has a question for its title and one short line under it, "About a third of Speaker 5's lines read like Speaker 2. Yes settles 58 lines." Nothing else: no arithmetic, no list of other speakers, no reasoning.

Biggest first. A proposal about a whole speaker ("the same voice?") settles dozens of lines, so those sit at the top. A proposal about a few lines ("are these 5 lines Speaker 1's?") settles a few, so those follow, the ones the voice backs first with the filled button. A merge and a move are different things underneath, and the page keeps them so (Undo works as now), but the person only ever answers yes or no.

The small ones fold. The 32 single lines the voice could not judge sit under one line with their count, and the 30 proposals that a speaker question would settle sit under another. The page is six cards and two folds instead of 44 cards.

The count at the top goes down as you answer.
