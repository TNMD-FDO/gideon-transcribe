The page keeps its shape: head, wall, transport, strip on the left; the tabs on the right. Four things change.

1. The focus camera is a player. The picture sits at its own proportions with no black bars, and the controls a person expects from a video sit on the picture when the pointer is over it: a scrub bar, play, back and forward, the time, which camera you hear, the speed, and fill the window. They fade when the pointer leaves. The transport row under the wall stays for people who like it, so nothing is taken away.

2. What is being said. The space under the tiles that was black now shows the focus camera's transcript, following the clock: the line being said is lit, two lines before and one after are there for context, and the band scrolls as the video plays. Press a line and every camera goes there. When the pointer is on the lit line, Mark and Clip from here appear on it. Change the focus and the band follows the new camera.

3. Clip is a button. One Clip button on the transport, beside the time, in the accent colour. Press it and the clip starts at this moment; the next drawing shows what happens then. The strip gains a Clips lane so a clip you made is visible where it lives in time, and the foot line says the ruler drag in one sentence.

4. A drag handle between the wall and the panel. Drag it and the panel grows or shrinks; the wall reflows; the width is remembered for you on this page. Double-press it to go back to the usual split.

5. The panel is the same panel, tabs and all, so nothing learnt is lost.

6. The strip is as it was, plus two things: a clip track under the ruler that says in words what it is for, hatched and dashed so it reads as a place to drag, with the scissors; and a Clips lane so a clip you made is visible where it lives in time. The old foot paragraph goes.

What the page looks like after Clip is pressed. Nothing opens over the video; the page itself shows the clip growing.

1. The Clip button becomes End the clip here, in the clip's yellow, with the running length beside it and Esc to cancel. Press it and the clip's end is set. The video keeps playing the whole time, so a person watches to the end of the moment and presses once.

2. On the strip, the span so far is a yellow band from the start to the clock, with a dashed edge at the moving end. The same band is on the player's scrub bar.

3. The clip box opens in the panel as the clip starts, with From filled in, To counting, the cameras ticked from what is on the wall, and Make the clip greyed until the end is set. Name is optional and offered from the nearest event. Make the clip does what Clip this event does today: one file, the picture order, landing on the Clips page and the new Clips lane.

4. The three other ways to start a clip stay and now say so: Clip this event on a chronology row, Clip from here on a transcript line, and dragging across the clip track. All four end in the same box.

The strip's ruler was a plain row of times that happened to accept a drag; nothing said so. The clip track is a band of its own under the times, and it says what it does.

At rest it is hatched in the clip's yellow with a dashed edge, the scissors and the words Drag across here to make a clip on the left, and the other ways to clip on the right in grey. The pointer over it becomes a crosshair, the edge turns solid and glows a little, and a faint yellow line follows the pointer down through the lanes so the time it is over is plain.

While dragging, the span so far fills in yellow on the track and as a ghost on the Clips lane, a tag follows the pointer with the start, the end and the length, and the words change to Dragging: release to set the end. Release, and the clip box opens in the panel with From and To filled in, exactly as it does when the Clip button is pressed. Esc while dragging cancels.

Once a clip exists it sits on the Clips lane; the track stays empty and ready, so the invitation is always there.

The same page with the panel pulled out to 640 px for reading the memo or the report. Everything on the left gives way.

1. The handle is where the hand went. While dragging, a faint line shows where the split will land, and the width snaps gently at the usual split and at half.

2. The focus camera shrinks with the column, still at its proportions; the tiles stay in one row until the column is too narrow for three, then wrap to two.

3. The words band and the strip shrink with the column and keep working. Below about 420 px for the wall, the transport wraps to two rows, and below that the app stops the drag: the video is never squeezed into a postage stamp.

Narrower works the same way in reverse. At 360 px the panel's tabs become icons with their names on hover, and the count on Report stays.
