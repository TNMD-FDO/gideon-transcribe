A

The same page, tightened

Nothing moves; everything that was tall gets short. The page head becomes one line (Back to the case at the left, the case's name in the title, the counts beside it, Export at the right). The browser's own player bar goes: Play and the time join the page's bar, which also carries "Note at this moment". The call's facts sit on one line. On the right each recording is one line with its note count, and each note shows the line it sits on in grey under it.

Room gained at 768 high: ten lines of transcript where there were four, the player and the waveform still in view, the first four notes on the right without scrolling.

What it keeps: the shape people have learned since v1.119.0, the keys, the Find layer, the pop-out. The smallest change and the quickest to build.

B

The notes are the page

The panes swap. The notes become the wide left column, a reading list in call order: each note in bold, the line it sits on quoted under it, who wrote it and when. Pressing a note plays its moment in the narrower player on the right, which shows the lines around it with the notes inline, as the Preview does. The Recordings and Details tabs move to buttons in the head, since they are visited, not read.

Room gained: the whole height for the notes, nine in view at once instead of four; the player loses nothing it needs.

What it changes: the page's job is reading what the team wrote, so that is what is big. Stepping with J and K still plays each moment. A bigger change than A, with the same parts rearranged.

C

The case on a strip

A strip across the top draws the whole case: one block per recording in call order, sized to its length, with a tick for every note. You see at a glance which calls the team has marked and which are untouched, and press a tick to go there. Under it three columns: the recordings with their note counts and reviewed marks, the lines with the player folded into the call's own bar, and this call's notes with the next ones on other calls.

Room gained: the head and the player each become one bar; eight lines in view with the whole case still in sight above them.

What it changes: the most. A new piece (the strip) and three columns, so it wants 1280 or wider; below that the strip stays and the columns fold to A's two. The best answer to "where are the notes in this case" and the biggest build.

The strip draws the cameras by the incident's clock

Where the recordings are cameras of an incident, C's strip lays them along the incident's clock instead of end to end, so a tick on BWC-1 and a tick on BWC-2 at the same moment sit one above the other. The middle column shows the picture at its width (about 300 px tall) over the lines, and the right column adds "At this moment on other cameras", the Notes page's own small All cameras.

What a video costs here: the lines fall from eight to four under the picture; the strip and the two side columns are untouched.

Where it shines: a multi-camera incident with notes on three cameras, which is the case this page was not drawn for the first time round.
Choosing with videos in mind: A keeps the most transcript in view with a video open (eight lines) because the picture goes beside the lines rather than above them; B keeps the notes list whole and lets the player column take the picture; C is the only one that shows where the notes fall across several cameras at once. Any of the three can borrow A's side-by-side picture for its player when the column is wide enough.
In all three: Back to the case at the head's left, the case's name in the title, one player bar instead of two, "Note at this moment" said in words, one line per recording on the right, and the page's keys (J, K, N, R) unchanged. A and C are a Phase 9 chapter 4 amendment of layout only; B amends which pane is which and would be drawn once more at the phone and tablet shapes before it is built.
