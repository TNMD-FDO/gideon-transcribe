Gideon Transcribe, a mock-up

The Speakers page, guided: one question at a time, its context beside it, and the way forward always in the same place

Drawn 2026-10-21, second drawing, after the maintainer's word that the first still bunched: this one is designed from the user's seat, for a process that is complex (six voices, four hundred lines, suggestions from three places, a phone system, a player and lanes) and must not feel so. Every name, call and line here is made up.

What a user has to do on a jail call is three things in order: say who each voice is, settle the lines the app is unsure of, and finish. So the page is those three steps, and inside each step it asks one question at a time: this voice, then the next; this line, then the next. Everything else (the whole transcript, the lanes, swap, zoom) is context or a tool, shown beside the question or kept under a button, never in the way.

What does not move. The recording's Speakers page stays the one place a proposal is accepted or declined; the number keys, not this voice, Name or merge, Undo, Open in a window, Done, the audit rows, the ratings and the re-read are as they are. The Case tab stays an overview plus Unlearn.

The six rules the page keeps

What a user can always see and always do, on every screen of the process

Where am I. The step (1 of 3, its name) and the count inside it ("Voice 2 of 6", "Line 3 of 39") in the guide at the top, with a bar per step that fills.
What do I do now. One plain sentence under the step, written for this screen ("Listen, then say who this is"), never a paragraph.
What comes next. One primary button at the right of the guide, always the same place, always the one thing that moves you on ("Next voice", "Next line", "Finish").
How do I go back. Back beside the primary, Undo beside Back, and every finished step a button in the guide. Nothing is a dead end.
One question, its context. The question on the left at a fixed width; on the right, only what bears on it: this voice's lines, or this line with the two before and after. The full transcript is a press away, not on the page.
Tools out of the way. The player is a bar at the foot with Play, the time and a scrub line; lanes, zoom, Swap two speakers and Open in a window sit under one "Tools" button. Someone who knows the page finds them in one press; someone who does not never meets them.

The guide is the page's one fixed thing. Top left: the step, its bar, the voice count, and the cast as chips that tick as they are named (the lit chip is the one on screen; any chip is a press to that voice). Top right: Undo, Back, and the one primary. Under them: the sentence for this screen.

The voice is the question. A big play that plays the three samples in a row, the samples under it with "not this voice", then "Who is this?" and the answers ranked best first, each in plain words ("sounds very like", "sounds a bit like", "a name said on the call") with where it comes from, a play of the candidate's own voice, and one button. The best answer is lit; the last choice opens the picker for anyone else or a merge. Skip is quiet and allowed.

The context is only this voice's lines, lit, with the others dimmed and long stretches folded; the whole call is one press. No proposals here: step 1 is names.

The foot is the player: Play, the time, a scrub line, Tools (lanes, zoom, swap, open in a window), and "Done for now", which leaves the page at this step and brings you back to it.

The first screen of step 2 is a choice, not a list. Three numbers and one primary: accept the lines the voice and the words agree on, or go one by one. Most people press the primary and are left with the 27 that need an ear.

Then one line at a time. The line as the question, with a big play; three rows that read as a sentence each (now marked, the voice says, the words say); the answers as buttons with the candidate's own voice to hear; "Leave it for now" quiet below. The context is the two lines before and the two after, the line itself outlined. Next line moves on whether or not you answered.

The kinds of doubt become wording, not boxes: "the voice and the words disagree", "the voice cannot tell", "two voices in one line" (with Split at the word as the primary), each in the guide's sentence.

Nothing is bunched because nothing shares the screen. One voice, or one line, or the receipt; the guide as one line and a sentence; Back, play and the primary fixed at the foot where thumbs are. The context ("their lines", "around this line") is a sheet that slides up and away. Tools are not on a phone at all: the lanes and swap are desk work.

The same words everywhere. "It's Riley", "Keep Sam", "Leave it", "Next voice", "Next line", "Finish": a person who learnt the page on a desk knows the phone, and the other way round.

Its lines never move, never take a name, stay out of the check, and the memo says the call system announced them. A line given to it by voice alone carries "not the system" in case the voice was wrong.

Why it blends into Speaker 1 today. The diarizer groups by similarity across the whole call and the system's recorded voice is one more voice to it. With the phrase lines as an anchor, the app pulls the rest of its cluster out at the voices-first run: lines whose print sits far from every party's and near the phrase lines' own. ADR 0021 promised this half; it is built here and measured on the office's jail calls before the switch is turned.

Last in the walk, never a question. It appears once, at the end of step 1, as a screen that says what it is; its chip in the guide is grey and never ticks.

What this is not

Left as it is, and what is kept

No new judgement. Every candidate on a voice screen exists today (the case's print, the unit's book, the words' hint); the screen only ranks them in one list with one wording. The ratings, the thresholds, the re-read, the stages' jobs and the audit rows do not change. "Sounds very like" is high, "sounds a bit like" is medium; low is never shown.

The one new finding is the phone system by voice, promised in ADR 0021, decided by the same rule as a hand-over, with "not the system" as the way back.

The old page's tools are all still there, under Tools: the lanes as the scrub bar, zoom, Swap two speakers, Open in a window, show the whole call. Someone who worked the old page loses nothing; someone new never has to find them.

Built in two releases, if chosen: the guide, the voice screen and the line screen for every recording first (words first included), then the stages as steps and the phone system's voice for jail calls, each walked on the server's calls before the next.
