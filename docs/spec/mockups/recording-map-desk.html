The map, cleaned up

The ask (2026-11-09, after v1.224.1): the Map tab shows up now, but the layout does not feel natural; the whole transcript should track in a panel along with the dots on the map, so any line is a press away (the Said here list of seven lines is not that); and other recordings should be on the map only when chosen.

What is on the page today (figure 1) and why it does not sit right
  1. The Map tab replaces the Transcript. The map gets a Said here list of seven lines beside it, a second, lesser transcript: no notes, no clips, no edits, no scrolling to a line of choice.
  2. The map's bar is the case Map tab's bar laid under the map: a count, This camera, Markers | Trails | Paths, six event ticks, Fit all, Tidy names, Save a picture. Three rows of controls under a map that is itself only 300 px tall on a laptop.
  3. Every camera of the day is drawn from the start. On a stop with five cameras the reader's own camera is one dot among five, and the others' names and trails crowd it.
  4. The Now caption is a fourth row under the bar, far from the map it describes.

Two directions follow for where the map lives, then the pieces they share: this camera alone with an Others menu, one slim bar on the map, the Now line on the map, and the whole transcript tracking with the dots. The words are made up.

Figure 2. Direction A: the map joins the picture in the stage; the Transcript keeps its whole column

Pressing Map no longer swaps the words away. The map takes its place in the stage under the picture and the transport, the Now line becomes the map's own caption, and the Transcript tab stays where it is with every line, note, clip and edit it has: it tracks the sound as it always has, and now its lines are the dots. The reader presses any line to go there; the marker and the filled pip move with it. On a laptop the stage is 400 px wide: the picture, then the transport, then a 300 px map; on the ultrawide the stage is 740 px and the map tall. The Map tab becomes a switch in the stage (Picture · Map · Both) rather than a tab that hides the words; the picture can also pop out as today, which leaves the whole stage to the map.

Figure 4. One bar on the map, and Others

Left to right: This camera (lit; a press centres on it and follows it again), Others ▾ (the other cameras of the day, none chosen to start; a tick draws one, grey, with its distance from this camera; All and None; the choice remembered for the case), then Trails ▾ (Markers only · Trails · Whole paths), Events ▾ (the six ticks that were a row of their own), and ⋯ (Fit, Tidy names, Save a picture, the readings). The camera's events still draw as pips of their own kind when ticked. On a recording with no file of its own (the "around" shape) Others starts with every camera chosen, since that is what the map is for there, and the bar says so.

Figure 5. The lines and the dots, one thing

The transcript's own lines carry the map: every line said where the camera had a fix is a pip on the path; the line being spoken is the filled pip and the lit line; a press on a line seeks the sound, and the marker and the pip move; a press on a pip lights the line and scrolls the transcript to it, with Following paused as any press pauses it and Resume a press away. A hovered pip shows its line; a hovered line shows nothing new, since it is the transcript. A line said with no fix has no pip and nothing else changes for it. The timeline's ticks at the lines' starts stay.

A press on a line
  1. the sound seeks there, Following on;
  2. the line lights as today;
  3. the marker moves to where the camera was;
  4. the pip of that line fills, the last one empties;
  5. the map pans if the marker left the view.
A press on a pip
  1. the sound seeks to the line's start;
  2. the transcript scrolls to the line and lights it;
  3. the pip fills;
  4. Following resumes from there.

My pick: Direction A, the map in the stage with the picture. It answers all three asks at once: the whole transcript tracks because it is the transcript, untouched, in its own column; the layout is natural because everything about the camera (its picture, its transport, its line, its place) is one column and everything said is the other; and the map starts with this camera alone, Others a menu away. It also restores what figure 4 of the first drawing showed for the ultrawide, the picture and the map stacked in the stage. The cost is a smaller map on a laptop (400 by about 300 px) unless the picture is popped out or the switch is set to Map alone; the ultrawide's 740 px stage has room for both. Direction B keeps the larger map at the price of narrower words on a laptop, and still leaves the Map tab as a mode to be in rather than a view that is simply there.

If A is taken, the Map tab goes (its panel, the Said here list and the map-beside-the-transcript of v1.224.0 with it), the stage gains the switch Picture · Map · Both with Both remembered as the default where there is a map, the map's bar and the Others menu are built as figure 4, and the transcript's lines gain the pips' behaviour of figure 5. The four shapes of the tab become the four shapes of the stage's switch: no switch for a recording without a map, "around" starting with Others all chosen, "file waiting" with the card above the map.
