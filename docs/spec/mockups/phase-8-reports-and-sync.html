What is wrong. Two styles share one name. The sheet's table is called inc-sync, and so is the one-line sync bar on each camera tile, which is a flexible wrapping row. The tile's rule wins, so the sheet's table is laid out as a wrapping row: five columns 712 pixels wide inside a 430 pixel panel, the actions column off the edge, and when an unsynced camera's controls open the rows fold into each other. It has been so since v1.66.0 and shows itself on a laptop with many cameras.

The fix. The tile's style gets the tile's name, and the sheet stops being a table: one card per camera, the shape the Cameras tab already uses, with the name and the placed pill on the first line, the clock and who placed it on the second, and the controls on a third only when the camera needs a hand. It fits the panel at any width, and eleven cameras read as a list. No mock-up needed beyond this; it is the Cameras tab's card.

Why it confuses. Two dropdowns both saying "none", a rule in small print, and no word on what the choice changes. The person has to know that a document's home decides which Report tab it appears on and which record the Comparison reads it against.

Proposed. One question, "This report is about", answered by two plain choices that say what each means and where the report lands. Coming from an incident's Details tab, the incident is already chosen and named, so the usual case is one press. The camera choice opens its picker only when chosen. The spec's "both" case (a report about the incident and one camera in particular) was never the common one; it stays reachable by adding the same PDF to the camera as well, and the rule line says so only if you need it.

Nothing changes underneath: the same two fields are posted, the same limits apply, the Documents tab's "The report for" column reads the same.

Why it jumbles. The Report tab was drawn for one report. With three, it draws them end to end, pages and paragraphs, and the panel becomes fifty pages of scroll with no landmark; the comparison button of the second document is somewhere in the middle.

Proposed. A row of pills at the top of the tab, one per document with its page count, the first open. Press a pill and that document's pages and paragraphs take the panel, with its own comparison button in its head. A citation from Gideon or a Search hit that lands in another document switches the pill for you, so the lit paragraph is always on screen. One document at a time is also what the Comparison reads, so the tab and the comparison agree.

The recording page's Report tab gets the same row when a recording has more than one document; with one, no row, as today.
