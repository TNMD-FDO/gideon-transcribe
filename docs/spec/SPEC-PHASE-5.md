# Gideon Transcribe, Phase 5 specification

The Speakers release, published as `v1.50.0`; chapter 3, the Speaker check, built as `v1.55.0`; chapter 4, the diarizer, written 2026-09-24 for `v1.80.0`.

## About this document

This is the build specification for Phase 5 of Gideon Transcribe: managing Speakers as a job of its own. A Transcript arrives with its Speakers labelled by the engine, Speaker 1 and Speaker 2 and on, and someone has to listen and say who each one is. Phase 1 gave that job a strip at the head of the Transcript and Phase 4's releases a window beside the page; the maintainer asked on 2026-09-12 for "more of a suite": a page whose one job is turning the engine's Speakers into people, with the Transcript in view while it happens. It builds on Phase 1 (`docs/spec/SPEC-PHASE-1.md`, the Transcript viewer and player chapter and the AI assistant chapter), Phase 2 (`docs/spec/SPEC-PHASE-2.md`, People and the Speakers tab), and Phase 4 (`docs/spec/SPEC-PHASE-4.md`), and changes nothing in them beyond what "What changes from earlier phases" lists.

Read it with the same companions: `CONTEXT.md` (the glossary, with the Phase 5 terms Speakers page, Lane, Sample, Voice print, Speaker check, and Speaker correction), `docs/spec/ADMIN-SETTINGS-CATALOGUE.md` (unchanged by chapter 1; chapter 3 adds the Speakers page), and `docs/whisperx-api.md` (unchanged by chapters 1 and 3; chapter 2 will open its door for embeddings).

Nothing in this document is office-specific. No new environment key is needed and no new service. The conventions of the Phase 1 document apply here unchanged.

## What Phase 5 adds

- **The Speakers page** (chapter 1, v1.50.0): a page of its own for one Recording, reached by Manage speakers on the Speakers strip and left by Done, that plays the Recording, lists its Speakers as cards with three Samples each, shows a Lane per Speaker across the whole Recording, and keeps the Transcript's Ledger under them filtered to the Speaker in hand. Naming offers the Case's People first. Rename, merge, giving one line to a Speaker, Suggest names, and Undo all live on it. The Speakers window of v1.47.0 to v1.49.0 becomes this page opened in a window.
- **Voice prints** (chapter 2, a later release): the app recognising a voice it has heard before in the same Case, by sound rather than words, and offering the name first. Written here as the reservation the page keeps for it and the rules it will have to meet; not built, and the earlier phases' rule against embeddings stands until the chapter is amended into force.

- **The Speaker check** (chapter 3, v1.55.0): after a Transcript lands with its Speakers told apart, the engine reads it in windows and proposes the lines whose words show they were given to the wrong Speaker. Every proposal is checked by the app, listed on the Speakers page as a Speaker correction with the words, the time and the reason, and moves a line only when a person accepts it, through the same move the number keys make. Shipped Off; a Speakers page in the Panel's Settings group holds its five settings.

- **The diarizer** (chapter 4, for the build, `v1.80.0`): the model that tells voices apart becomes a Panel choice, Nemotron 3 Diarization by default (open, ungated, up to eight voices) in a container of its own, pyannote kept one setting away; the speaker-count hint and the embeddings stay pyannote's; one result shape whichever runs.

Chapter 1 adds no admin setting, no audit row, no table, and no environment key. Chapter 4 adds one setting, no audit row and no table, and a second container in the WhisperX service's Compose file. Chapter 3 adds a Speakers settings page, five audit rows, and two tables.

## 1. The Speakers page

Written 2026-09-12 from the maintainer's ask ("treat any in depth speaker management as more of a suite... a separate window/function/feature set... I imagine they wanna see the transcript as well") and the answers to the build's questions the same day: the page serves a deliberate pass after processing and a quick fix while reviewing about equally; naming the Speakers is the first job; it is a full page of its own; and the Case's People are offered first. The layout is the maintainer's pick from the mockups, "the line-up with lanes": Idea 1 (cards down the left, the recording and the Ledger on the right) with Idea 3's Lanes folded in as the page's timeline.

### Principles

1. **One page, one job.** The page exists to say who each Speaker is. Everything on it serves that: hearing a Speaker, seeing where in the Recording they talk, naming them, joining two the engine split, and fixing a line given to the wrong one. It does not correct text, mark Clips, summarise, or chat; those stay on the recording page, one press away.
2. **The Transcript stays in view.** The Ledger, at full height in the page's middle column since v1.55.1 (under the Lanes until then), is the viewer's own Ledger, the same rows in the same colours, so nothing about a line has to be remembered from another page. Follow keeps the line being spoken in view while the Recording plays.
3. **Both tempos.** A deliberate pass reads the progress line, works down the cards, and presses Done. A quick fix opens the page, picks one card, names it, and presses Done; nothing on the page has to be finished first, and nothing is lost by leaving.
4. **The Case's People first.** Inside a Case, naming offers the Case's People with their Roles before a free name, so the same officer gets the same name across the Case's Recordings without typing. Outside a Case naming is free-form, as Phase 1 has it.
5. **Nothing new is stored for it.** Samples, Lanes, the progress line, and the fragment hint are computed from the Segments the Transcript already holds. The page adds no table and no setting. Every change it makes is a change the viewer already makes, through the viewer's own routes, with the viewer's own audit rows and the viewer's own Undo.
6. **The engine is not asked to listen.** Chapter 1 recognises nobody by sound. Suggest names on the page is the Phase 1 feature, from the words, on request, one call per press. Voice prints are chapter 2, not built.

### Words

**Speakers page**, **Lane**, and **Sample**, as `CONTEXT.md` defines them. The page says "speaker" for what the glossary calls a Speaker throughout; the mockups' word "voice" is not used on the page. **Voice print** is chapter 2's word and appears on the page only as the reservation below.

### In and out

- **Manage speakers** on the Speakers strip of the recording page opens the page at `recording/<id>/speakers`. It replaces Tag speakers (v1.47.0 to v1.49.0) on the strip. The strip's fold, the chips, and the Speakers toggle stay as Phase 1 has them.
- The page opens at the recording page's playhead when it was opened from there, paused. Its own player has the sound from then on; the recording page, if it is still open in another tab, is not kept in step (see Open in a window for the arrangement that is).
- **Done** in the page's head returns to the recording page at the page's playhead, the way a citation from a Case page opens the viewer at a time. The browser's Back does the same without the time.
- **Open in a window**, beside Done, opens the same page in a window of its own, which is what the Speakers window of v1.48.0 was: the window plays the Recording, the recording page follows it through the browser's channel, and a change made in the window redraws the page's rows, exactly as the Transcript viewer and player chapter's Speakers window paragraph has it. The route `recording/<id>/window/speakers` keeps working and shows this page. In a window the head has no Done; closing the window is Done.
- The page needs a Transcript with Speakers. A Recording without one (not diarized, one Side) has no Manage speakers on its strip, and the route shows the recording page's "This recording has no speaker labels to tag" line with a link back.
- Standing is the viewer's: whoever may open the Recording may open the page, and a Collaborator acts on it as the owner does (Phase 2 Sharing chapter).

### The head

One line across the top: **Speakers of <title>**, the progress line **N of M speakers named** with a small bar (a Speaker is named when its name is not its engine label; on a Two-channel call a Side labelled by its Side alone counts as unnamed), the last change with **Undo** when there is one ("Undo: merged Speaker 4 into Ofc. Reyes"), **Suggest names** under the AI assistant chapter's rules (present only while Speaker suggestions is On, two or more Speakers are unnamed, and the engine passes the minute check, greyed with the unavailable line otherwise), **Open in a window**, and **Done**.

### The cards

Down the left, one card per Speaker, in the order the Speakers first spoke, which is the order of their numbers.

- **The head of a card**: the Speaker's colour dot, the name in bold (or the engine label while unnamed), the Role pill inside a Case when the Person holds one, a **named** or **unnamed** pill, and the engine label in small type at the right so a Provenance reference can be matched (the Exports chapter's Appearances table prints both).
- **The line under it**: how many lines, how long talking (the sum of the Speaker's Segment lengths), and the time of the first line: "44 lines, 5 min 03 s talking, first at 0:07".
- **Samples**: up to three play buttons, each labelled with its time. The Samples are chosen from the Speaker's own lines: one from the first third of them, one from the middle third, one from the last third, preferring in each third the line nearest four seconds long among those between two and eight seconds, and taking the longest when none is; a Speaker with fewer than three lines shows what it has. A line whose timing is a guess (v1.141.0: the recogniser repeated a phrase of up to three words three times in a row, or spread the words to under three tenths of the recording's usual pace over three seconds or more) is passed over while any sure line remains, and in the transcript carries the quiet pill "the timing of these words is a guess", on an ordinary line and on a proposal alike. Pressing a Sample plays the Recording from half a second before that line's start and pauses half a second after its end (v1.141.0; the line alone before it), so a voice is heard on its own and whole; Space carries on from there. The transcript on the page lights each word of the line in hand as the voice reaches it (v1.141.0), as the recording page does. While the card is picked, its Samples are marked on its Lane. From v1.143.0 (the drawing `docs/spec/mockups/phase-5-speakers-page-breathes.html`, chosen 2026-10-20) the three Samples sit one to a line, each with its play, its time, the start of its words and "not this voice" beside it, so the words say which Sample is which before it is played. Where the voice listened (v1.145.0), a name the words point at is a **hint** on the card, evidence and not an answer: "The words point at a name: Riley", the quote with Play and whether it was said by this voice or to it, and Name or merge... opens with the name typed; a named card folds to one line until "change" opens it.
- **Name or merge...** (v1.143.0; until then **rename**, **same person as...** and the name box below). One button on the card, the card's primary while the Speaker is unnamed, which opens the speaker picker (chapter 3) beside the card asking "<Speaker> is". Another Speaker of the call is "the same person": picking one is the merge, with the Speakers strip's confirmation and audit rows. A Person of the Case names every line; Someone new types a name with a Role from the Admin's Speaker roles list and makes the Person and names the Speaker in one step; a name another Speaker already holds is the merge, confirmed as the merge is. The name the check suggested carries a "suggested" pill in the picker and is lit first, so Accept on the card and the picker agree. Nothing opens inside the card any more.
- **Until v1.143.0: rename, same person as... and the name box.** rename and same person as... were the Speakers strip's rename and merge; inside a Case, rename was the pick-or-type box of the Phase 2 People chapter. The name box on an unnamed Speaker's card was that box open on the card with **Name** as its button, inside a Case headed **Known in this case** with the Case's People first, each with its Role and "in N other recordings", then **New name "<typed>"** with a Role picker from the Admin's Speaker roles list (Phase 2 had the Role set on the Speakers tab only; see What changes). The picker's Someone new keeps that Role picker.
- **The fragment hint**: a Speaker whose lines never overlap in time with another Speaker's lines, and who has fewer lines than that other Speaker, carries the line "Never speaks while <other> does; see the lanes." with **Merge into <other>**. When more than one Speaker qualifies as the other, the one with the most lines is named. The hint says what the timing shows and no more; whether the two are one person is the listener's call, which is why the Samples and the Lanes sit beside it. Overlap is measured on the Segments as they are; two lines that touch within half a second are not an overlap.
- **The suggestion line**: after Suggest names, each suggestion lands on its Speaker's card as "Probably <name> (<Role>), from <time>: '<quote>'" with **Accept** and **Reject**, which are the Phase 1 Accept and Reject with their audit rows; the dashed pill on the Transcript's first Segment appears as well, since the Ledger is the viewer's. Only high and medium suggestions arrive, as the AI assistant chapter has it.
- **The reservation for chapter 2**: an unnamed Speaker's card keeps one line, empty and not drawn until chapter 2 is built, for "Sounds like <Person>" (see chapter 2). Nothing about it shows in v1.50.0.
- A card is **picked** by a press on it or on its Lane's head, or by pressing one of its Samples; the picked card is outlined, its Lane is outlined, and the Ledger filters to it. A number key never picks: it gives the line being spoken, as below. The first unnamed Speaker is picked when the page opens, or the first Speaker when all are named.
- Under the cards, one line: "1 to 9 give the line being spoken to a speaker. Space plays, B goes back three seconds, Left and Right five."

### The Lanes

On the right, between the transport and the Ledger, the page's timeline: a ruler with times across the top, then one Lane per Speaker in card order, each with its head (colour dot and name) at the left and its track across the width, with a block for every stretch that Speaker talks. The playhead is a line through every Lane.

- **The blocks** are the Speaker's Segments; Segments of one Speaker with less than a second between them are drawn as one block, so a Lane stays light on a long Recording. A block's title on hover is its times.
- **Seeking**: a press anywhere on a track seeks the player there; pressing on a block seeks to the block's start. The Lanes are the page's scrub bar and there is no other.
- **Zoom**: **all**, **2 min**, and **30 s** at the right of the Lanes; the two narrower views keep the playhead in view and scroll with it. All is the default and is remembered in the browser.
- **The picked Speaker's Lane** is outlined in its colour, and its Samples are marked on it as small ticks.
- **Drag to merge**: dragging one Lane's head onto another's is the strip's merge, with the same confirmation. The keyboard's route to the same thing is same person as... on the card.
- **Many Speakers**: with more than six Speakers, those who talk for under a minute in all fold into one Lane headed "N small speakers", which a press expands into their Lanes and a second press folds again. Their cards are unaffected.
- **A Two-channel call** has one Lane per Speaker as any Recording has, the Side's name in the head where Phase 1 labels a Speaker by its Side.
- A sound-only Recording has the Lanes where a video has them; the picture above them is the waveform strip.

### The player and the Ledger

- **The player** is the viewer's: the picture (or the waveform strip on a sound Recording) at the top of the right column, the transport under it (Play and pause, back three seconds, five seconds either way, the clock, the speed), and the page's keys. On a window narrower than 1280 pixels the picture shrinks to a strip the way the stage does on the recording page, so the Lanes and the Ledger keep their room; the cards move above the Lanes in one column.
- **The Ledger** is the viewer's Transcript, the same rows at the same size in the same colours (the Transcript viewer and player chapter's Ledger of v1.46.0), without the marking, correcting, describing, and Clip controls, which belong to the recording page. Above it, a filter with two pills: **<picked speaker> only**, the default whenever a card is picked, which shows that Speaker's lines with the line before and after each dimmed for context, and **Everyone**. Follow is on and keeps the line being spoken in view under the viewer's own band rule; the pill to turn it off is the viewer's.
- **The line being spoken** carries its actions: play, and **not this speaker**, which opens the list of the other Speakers and gives the line to the one picked. That is the Speakers window's number key by mouse: one Segment changes hands, the row Speaker changed on a line is written (no name), and Undo puts that one line back. The number keys 1 to 9 do the same for the line being spoken, as the window's did; there is no key past 9, and the list covers the rest.
- A press on a row seeks to it, as on the recording page.

### Undo

The Transcript's twenty-deep record of renames, merges, and lines given (the Transcript viewer and player chapter, v1.36.0 and v1.47.0) is the page's Undo, shown in the head with the last change in words. Undo on the page is the strip's Undo.

### What changes from earlier phases

- **Phase 1, the Transcript viewer and player chapter, the Speakers window**: Tag speakers on the strip becomes Manage speakers and opens the Speakers page; the Speakers window is the Speakers page opened in a window, with the following and the channel as that paragraph has them. The window's own roster, nowbox, and number keys are the page's cards, line being spoken, and number keys. Its route stays.
- **Phase 1, the Transcript viewer and player chapter, Speaker suggestions**: Suggest names is offered on the Speakers page as well as on the strip, under the same rules; a suggestion shows on the card as well as in the pill.
- **Phase 2, the People chapter, In the viewer inside a Case**: the pick-or-type box on the Speakers page offers a Role picker beside New name, so a Person made from the page can take its Role at once. The Speakers tab's Edit stays the place to change a Role afterwards. Accepting a role suggestion still creates or joins a Person of that name without setting the Role, as Phase 2 has it.
- **Phase 2, the People chapter, Principles 4**, and **Phase 1's ruled-out item 12** (no embeddings requested or stored): unchanged by chapter 1. Chapter 2 is written as the amendment that will change them, and is not in force.
- Nothing changes for the Speakers tab on the Case page, the strip's chips, exports, Clips, Summary, Chat, or Moments.

### Audit rows

None new in v1.50.0; Speakers swapped since v1.56.0. The page writes the rows the strip and the window write: Speaker renamed, Speakers merged, Speakers swapped, Speaker changed on a line, Speaker suggestion accepted and rejected, and the AI assistant call for `speaker_suggestions`; and, inside a Case, the People chapter's rows when a Person is made, joined, or merged. None holds a name.

### Settings

None new. Speaker suggestions (Phase 1) governs Suggest names on the page; Speaker roles (Phase 2) fills the Role picker.

### Not in this phase

- **Voice prints** (chapter 2).
- **Splitting a line** between two Speakers at a word. A line that holds two voices is given to the one who says more of it; the maintainer did not ask for the cut.
- **Checking off** a Speaker or a stretch of lines as verified; nothing marks what a person has listened to.
- **Lanes on the recording page.** The Timeline there stays as it is.
- **A page across a Case.** The page is one Recording's; the Case page's Speakers tab remains the view across Recordings.

### Left to the build

- The Samples' choice within the rule above (which line in a third when several are between two and eight seconds), and the tick marks' shape on a Lane.
- How the block list reaches the page: from the Segments the page already renders, or a lighter list of spans in the state answer. Either way nothing is stored.
- The narrow-window arrangement below 1280 pixels, within the rule that the cards go above the Lanes and the picture gives way first.
- The exact wording of the fragment hint and the "not this speaker" list, within the words fixed here.

## 2. Voice prints (coming into force with the voice plan's V4; the rules)

**In force from v1.144.0** (the voice plan's fourth release, built 2026-10-21 to the drawing `docs/spec/mockups/phase-5-voice-prints.html`, chosen the same day). The maintainer chose on 2026-09-12 to build the page first and voice prints in a later release, and this chapter was the reservation the page keeps and the rules the release would have to meet. On 2026-10-19, after the measurement in `docs/research/speaker-verification.md`, the maintainer decided (ADR 0021) that the chapter comes into force as written, with the additions under "What the voice plan adds". Phase 1's ruled-out item 12 and Phase 2's Principle 4 are amended by this release (see "What it will change"); the Admin's switch is Off as shipped, and for an office that leaves it off nothing here is in force.

### What it is

Recognising a Speaker by sound: the app noticing that the voice of an unnamed Speaker in this Recording is the voice of a Person already named in another Recording of the same Case, and offering that name on the card before anyone listens, as "Sounds like <Person> (<Role>)" with Accept, the way Suggest names offers a name from the words.

### What the app already has

The WhisperX service returns, on request, one vector per Speaker from its diarization model (`docs/whisperx-api.md`, `return_speaker_embeddings`). The app asks for them today for the stretches of a Live recording and matches labels across stretches by a same-voice threshold (Phase 3, the Live recording chapter), then discards them. Nothing is kept. From chapter 4 (v1.80.0) the vectors are pyannote's alone: the Nemotron diarizer, the default, returns spans and no embeddings, so voice prints need an embedding step of their own. That step is the diarizer container's second duty (chapter 4, ADR 0021): TitaNet-large, a speaker verification model loaded through the NeMo the container already pins, returning one vector per stretch and one per label on request.

### The rules it will have to meet

1. **A Voice print is a Person's, inside a Case, and nowhere else.** One vector per Person per Case, kept as a field of the Person, made when a Speaker is named inside the Case from that Speaker's vector, and deleted with the Person. Nothing links a voice across Cases, as Phase 2's Principle 2 has it for names. **Amended v1.146.0:** nothing crosses a Case on its own; by a person's choice a print may also be kept in the unit's voice book, for a Role the Admin allows, matched only by a press on a card: see "The unit's voice book" below.
2. **It is content.** A Voice print is biometric data about a client, a witness, or an officer. It is on the never-logged list, never exported, never in a Backup's on-disk files beyond the database dump, and never sent to the engine or anywhere else. The service that makes it is local.
3. **On request, or at most as a suggestion.** Matching runs when a Transcript lands inside a Case with unnamed Speakers, and produces suggestions only; nothing is named by itself. A suggestion carries how close the match was, in the AI chapter's three words, and only the two upper ones show.
4. **Off by default.** An admin setting turns it on, and turning it off deletes every Voice print the Case holds; the pages say so before the switch is thrown.
5. **Honest wording.** "Sounds like" and never "is"; the card keeps the Samples beside it so a person listens before accepting.

### What the voice plan adds (ADR 0021, 2026-10-19)

- **Learnt from acceptances only.** A Person's print is made when a person accepts a match or names a Speaker inside the Case, from that Speaker's vector, and refined by each later recording a person accepts a match on; nothing a person has not accepted teaches it. A print taught by one recording rates no higher than medium.
- **Two acts, recorded apart.** The run may propose both on one card, but the app records "this voice is the same as that Speaker" and "that Speaker is this Person" as two facts, so a wrong name is one rename and a wrong grouping is one unlearn.
- **Unlearn**, on the Case's Speakers tab beside the Person: deletes the Person's print and returns every Speaker it matched to unnamed and marked, with the chain of what it undid shown before it is pressed.
- **Sounds like, rated by the weakest link.** A match's rating is the lowest of the print's own confidence, the recording's calibration and the stretch's length, in the AI chapter's three words; only the two upper ones show; the card keeps the samples beside it. One more link (v1.150.0): a print taught on recordings of one type and matched on a Recording of another (a body camera's print on a jail call) rates medium at most, and the match carries one sentence saying which kinds taught it and that a different kind of recording changes a voice; an untyped Recording, or a print taught on untyped ones, is not judged. On the guided page a conflict in which no candidate is better than medium never leads the screen: Choose... is the primary and the weak matches fold under "N weak matches in conflict, listen before choosing".
- **The Case's Speakers tab** gains the run's proposals grouped by Person, conflicts (two People matched to one voice), the chain on every name, Unlearn, and Decided so far per Person and per source; a dismissal alarm when a Person's matches are dismissed again and again.
- **The Side rule's check.** On a two-channel call named across the Case (Phase 9 chapter 2), a Side whose voice does not sound like its Person is flagged on the card, never renamed.
- **The Call system has no print**, anywhere: it is found by its phrases and by sounding unlike every party on the recording itself.

### The pages (v1.144.0; the drawing `docs/spec/mockups/phase-5-voice-prints.html`)

Two rules from 2026-10-19 shape them: the recording's Speakers page is the one place a proposal is accepted or declined, and a Sounds like is a proposal, so it sits on the card with the voice under the ear; the Case's Speakers tab is an overview and the home of Unlearn, and asks no question the card has not already asked.

- **The card** (figure 1). On an unnamed Speaker's card, under its Samples, a block: "Sounds like <Person>" with the Role pill and the rating, a Preview button per recording that taught the print ("Dana on Call 1, 0:48", the floating Preview of v1.98.0), the line "the samples above are this voice", **Accept: <label> is <Person>** and **Not them**, and one quiet sentence: Accept names every line on this call and teaches the print from it; Undo takes the name back and the print forgets this call. The rating is the weakest link of the score, the print's own confidence (one recording rates medium at best) and the voice heard (under ten seconds rates medium at best); low is never made. Behind Accept the app records two facts apart (the voice grouped, the Speaker named), so a wrong name is one rename and a wrong grouping is one Unlearn. Not them sets the match aside for this recording and teaches nothing. The picker's Name or merge... carries a "suggested" pill on the matched Person, lit first. The strip's pill on the recording page counts the voices waiting.
- **The conflict and the Side alarm** (figure 3). Two People over one voice: "Sounds like two people", a Preview per Person, a button per Person and **Neither**; nothing is proposed until a person has heard them all. On a two-channel call named across the Case by the Side rule, a Side whose print is far from its Person's carries "Does not sound like <Person>" with the voice disagreeing, their voice to play, **Not <Person> on this call...** (the picker for the whole side; the rule keeps naming the other calls) and **It is <Person>** (keeps the name and teaches the print from this call). Nothing is renamed by the voice alone.
- **The Case's Speakers tab** (figure 2). One new column, Voice: the print's state ("print from 3 recordings", "no print yet", or "voice unlearnt <date> by <who>"), a play of the voice, the Chain in one line, "matches rate medium at best" on a print from one recording, what waits with a link to the call's Speakers page that decides it, and under a Person whose matches were turned down three times in thirty days: "Matches to <Person> were turned down 3 times this month. If the print keeps being wrong, unlearn it and name them by hand once more." Below the table the Side rule's alarms as blocks pointing at their calls, **Decided so far in this case** per source (moves from the words, moves from the voice, hand-overs, names from the words, Sounds like), and **Sounds like, per person**. The tab never carries Accept. The lead line says how many people have a print and how many voices wait. **As people (v1.151.0; the drawing `docs/spec/mockups/phase-5-speakers-tab-and-voices.html`, figures 1 and 2):** the table becomes a strip and cards. First **what needs you**, one card each: voices still to name with the button that opens that recording's guided Speakers page, Sounds like waiting with the button to the card that decides it, the Side rule's alarm, and the Side rule itself as a quiet statement with its form folded; when nothing waits, one calm line. Then one card per Person, anyone with something waiting first and then by how much they are heard: the name and Role, the voice line (play opens the Preview in the page's right pane; "voice known · print from N recordings"; medium at best; "Kept for the <unit> unit" as one pill), where they appear as a chip per recording that opens it at their first line, the Chain in one line, the book's offer or sentence, the dismissal call-out, and what waits with the link to the call that decides it. Filter chips (all, voice known, no voice yet, kept for the unit, something waiting) show one kind; Add a person opens its form in place. Every action sits behind one menu on the card: Rename everywhere, Role and notes, Merge into, then apart from them Stop keeping, Unlearn their voice and Delete from this case, with the confirm boxes as they were. Decided so far and Sounds like per person fold under "Numbers"; the unnamed speakers fold under their own line, open while any wait.
- **Unlearn** (figure 4). The row's last, danger button, on a Person with a print, with the Chain above it; the box lists each recording by what happens to it: the recordings the voice named go back to unnamed and are marked "was <Person>, by voice" on their cards, the recordings named by hand stay, the matches waiting are withdrawn; an audit row of counts. Afterwards the Voice column reads "voice unlearnt <date> by <who>".
- **The switch** (figure 5). One Admin row, Remember voices across a case, in the Speakers settings beside the voice step's rows, Off as shipped, greyed while Cases are off. Off deletes every print, every kept label vector and every waiting match at once; the tray's note says how many people and cases before the switch is thrown; each Case says "voices were forgotten on <date>" until a print is learnt again. On again starts from nothing.

## 3. The Speaker check

Written 2026-09-14 from the maintainer's ask, after a run of the office's engine over three corrected recordings in one case ("the results are very promising! build it"), and the decisions taken in the same conversation: the check works on the separation, which lines belong to which voice, and not on naming, which Suggest names already does; it proposes and never applies; it can be turned on and off in the Panel with its settings beside it. The probe that preceded it is recorded in `docs/research/speaker-check-probe.md`.

### Principles

1. **Separation, not naming.** Suggest names says who a Speaker is. The check says which lines are whose. They sit side by side on the Speakers page and never overlap: the check works on the labels as they stand, and a Speaker already named is proposed by that name.
2. **It proposes; a person moves.** Nothing changes when the check runs. A Speaker correction is a proposal on the page until somebody presses Accept, and an accepted correction is a line move like any other: the viewer's own function, the viewer's own audit row, the viewer's own Undo.
3. **Only among the Speakers there are, and one more by a person's Yes.** A line may be moved only to a Speaker already on the Transcript. The engine is given that list and may answer with nothing else; the app checks every answer again. The check never merges two Speakers and never touches a word. From the voice plan's V2 (ADR 0021) it may propose a Hand-over, a second voice under one label from a time, which a person's Yes turns into a new Speaker by splitting the label there; the check itself adds nothing.
4. **What the words give, and what the voice says of it.** The check finds the moves the words make plain: a question and its answer under one label, a person addressed by name answering under the asker's label, a command under the label of the one it was given to. Where either Speaker could have said a line, nothing is proposed. From V2 the voice step gives its word on every proposal (agrees, against, cannot tell) and may propose a move the words never made; the fine separations neither can settle stay a person's.
5. **Off until judged.** Shipped Off. An office turns it on after seeing it do well on its own recordings, as with Suggest names.

### Words

Speaker check, Speaker correction, as the glossary has them. On the page: **Check the speakers**, **Suggested corrections**, **Accept**, **Dismiss**, **Accept all**. A correction reads "Speaker 2, not Speaker 1" with the words, the time, and the reason.

### When it runs

- **As the Transcript lands**, from the queue's hook, when the switch is on and the Recording was transcribed with its Speakers told apart and two or more Speakers came out of it. One run at a time per Transcript; nothing is queued twice.
- **Under the overnight position**, a run queued by day waits for the Vision window to open (the Vision page's Overnight from), so a shared engine is not asked by day. A run pressed on the page runs at once whatever the position.
- **On a press** of Check the speakers on the Speakers page, at any time, for a Transcript the check never ran on or after a merge or a rename changed the labels.
- **Process again** takes the corrections with the old Transcript, as it takes the name suggestions; the new Transcript is checked afresh as it lands.

### The run

**Voices first (v1.145.0; the drawing `docs/spec/mockups/phase-5-voices-first.html`, chosen 2026-10-21).** On a Recording whose type the voice listens to (Voices help the check), the run is turned over. As the Transcript lands the one job does the voice's work and no engine call for moves: the voice step reads every line's own words, builds each label's print and the Hand-overs, matches the unnamed voices against the Case's prints (Sounds like), and lists the **lines in doubt**: a line whose own print sits nearer another speaker's than its own (the voice's own proposals, under a cap of eighty), a line the voice cannot tell between two (under a cap of forty, folded on the page), and a line that carries two voices (a run of at least two words, a second or more, wearing another label at its end or its start: a proposal to split the line at that word). The names pass runs in the same job and what it finds is evidence on the cards, never an Accept. The page then has three stages: **who is speaking**, **lines in doubt**, **done**. At stage 2 the engine is asked one narrow question, the lines in doubt with two lines either side and the named cast with roles and Also called names: which of these speakers said each; its answer lands on each row as the words' word, agreeing with the voice, against it, or naming a third, and the voice's rows stand whatever the engine does. At stage 3 the check below runs once over the whole call against the named cast, and what it proposes replaces the voice's rows left undecided. The model is never asked about labels: both readings come after the names. Where the voice is off for the type, or could not be read, the run is as the next paragraphs describe, with no stages.

The engine's lane, one job. The Transcript is read as the assistant reads it, one numbered line per Segment with its time and its label, in windows of about ten minutes of talk (a setting), each window starting six lines before the one before it ended so a line at an edge keeps its question or its answer in view. Every window is one call: the Ground rules, the Speaker check template, the answer's shape, the list of Speakers, and the lines. The answer is a JSON list of moves, each a line number, the label as shown, the Speaker it belongs to, and a reason; vLLM's structured output holds the Speaker to the list given. Thinking is off; the sampling is the suggestions' own, deterministic.

The app then checks every move: the line is one of the window's, the Speaker is on the Transcript, the label is the one the engine was shown, and the move is to somebody else. One correction per line; the rest are dropped without a word to anyone. An unreadable window is a lost window, not a lost run. A problem mid-run fails the run with its reason and keeps what the earlier windows found, since every one passed the checks. A run replaces the pending corrections with its own and offers again nothing a person dismissed.

Chosen 2026-10-21 after the maintainer's word that the page had grown squished (six releases since v1.143.0 each put something on the same layout) and that it should be designed from the user's seat: a process that is complex and must not feel so. The page is the three things a user has to do, in order, and inside each it asks one question at a time.

- **The six rules the page keeps**, on every screen. Where am I: the step (1 of 3, its name) and the count inside it ("Voice 2 of 6", "Line 14 of 39") in the guide at the top, with a bar per step. What do I do now: one plain sentence under the step, written for this screen. What comes next: one primary at the right of the guide, always the same place ("Next voice", "Next line", "Finish"). How do I go back: Back beside the primary, Undo beside Back, every finished step a press back. One question with its context: the question on the left at a fixed width, on the right only what bears on it (this voice's lines; this line with the two before and after), the whole call a press away. Tools out of the way: the player a bar at the foot with Play, the time and a scrub line; everything else under one Tools button.
- **The steps.** On a recording the voice listens to, three: Who is speaking, Lines in doubt, Done, which are the stages of "Voices first" above, the stage on the run as before. On any other recording, two: Who is speaking, then The check's proposals, kept on the page alone.
- **Step 1, one voice at a time.** The cast as chips in the guide, ticked as they are named; the voice on screen with a big play that plays its samples in a row, the samples with "not this voice", then "Who is this?" and the answers ranked best first in plain words: "sounds very like" (high) and "sounds a bit like" (medium) from the case's prints and from the unit's book, "a name said on the call" from the words' hint, each with where it comes from, a play of the candidate's own voice, and one button; the last choice opens the picker for anyone else or a merge; Skip is quiet and allowed. A named voice is a screen that says so with Change; the phone system is a screen with nothing to answer. Nothing is named by a voice alone. The unit's book answers only by a press, as chapter 2 has it.
- **Step 2, one line at a time.** Where the voice listened, the first screen is a choice: the lines the voice and the words agree on, accept them all or go one by one. Then each line as the question, with a big play; now marked, the voice says, the words say, each a row that reads as a sentence; the answers as buttons ("It's Marcus", "Keep Sam", Someone else) with the candidate's own voice to hear; a mixed line offers Split at the word; "Leave it for now" quiet below. The context is the two lines before and after. On a words-first recording the same screen walks the check's proposals, with "Accept all the voice backs" as a quiet shortcut where a voice word exists.
- **Step 3, the receipt.** Voices named, lines decided, left to look at; "Look at the N left, one by one", "Run the check again", Finish. Back is a real way back: step 1 again names a voice late and the proposals catch up as the re-read does.
- **On a phone**, the same screens one column: the guide as one line and the sentence, the question filling the screen, the context a press away, Back, play and the primary at the foot.
- **This release and the next.** v1.148.0 carries the guide, the voice screen, the line screen, the receipt and the phone, for every recording, drawn from the check's state and the transcript and acting through the endpoints the page already had. The lanes, zoom and Swap two speakers stay on the page as it was, reached from Tools as "the classic page" at `/speakers/classic`, until the second release folds them into the guided page; the second release also brings the phone system found by its voice (ADR 0021's promise), measured on the office's jail calls before its switch is turned, and the window of Open in a window, which shows the classic page meanwhile.

### The clock verdict (ADR 0022, v1.151.0, and the re-read, ADR 0023, v1.152.0; the drawing `docs/spec/mockups/phase-5-preview-and-clock.html`)

The transcript's clock is checked against the audio's own energy as every Transcript lands, after the Call system's pass and before the check runs, on every Recording type, and again on Process again; a Transcript from before the release gets it once, when a check runs on it. No word changes; from v1.152.0 a marked block is placed again by the audio (ADR 0023), and until then nothing moves.

- **The three rules.** A hole the audio fills: eight seconds or more with no line where the audio has sound for over seven tenths of it. A line over a pause: a line of two seconds or more whose words are said to run across over a second of quiet inside it. The bound: a block of lines (the run between two long gaps) that has a line over a pause and a sound-filled hole after it is marked "the clock here may be off by up to N seconds", N the hole's length, never a guess beyond it. The pauses come from ffmpeg's silence detection on the prepared audio the recogniser was sent; the thresholds are the build's constants from the shape measured on the office's call and move only if a judgement sheet by ear says so. **The squeeze** (v1.152.0): of a block that qualifies, only the lines whose clock lies within forty-five seconds (the recogniser's window and a half) before the hole's end are marked, the line over a pause among them, and the first marked line heads the block; on the measured call the whole run back to the previous long gap had marked 280 of 433 lines, most of them in their place.
- **What a mark changes.** The recording page's transcript draws a marked block with a dotted rule down its left edge and one pill at its head ("clock unsure · up to 16 s early · heard about 2:21"; since v1.159.1 the pill reads "time uncertain" alone, at the maintainer's ask, with the bound, the time heard and the reason in its hover text and the numbers on the Details tab); play on a marked line runs from half a second before its own clock to the end of the hole beside it, and the button says so. The guided page's line screen says the clock is unsure in the guide's sentence, shows a sound strip under the question (the window being played, the line's own clock, where the sound is) and says in the voice row that the voice did not measure the line. The Samples never come from a marked line while any sure line remains, and a card says how many lines were left out. The voice step gives a marked line no vector: no word on it, no print taught from it, no match rated by it. The "hear" buttons, the picker's plays and the Preview seek to a line whose clock is sure while any remains, and a Preview opened on a marked line plays to where the sound is and names the verdict.
- **The re-read (v1.152.0; ADR 0023).** A marked block is placed again by the audio, not slid: on the measured call a line early in the block was eight seconds early and a line late in it sixteen, so neither a shift nor an even stretch would match. When a landing or a late pass marks any block, the app cuts that window of the prepared audio (a second before the first marked line to half a second after the hole's end), sends it to the WhisperX service as small jobs of its own (the recording's task, model and language, no speakers, the same vocabulary, the speakers pass's priority, behind a Live recording's stretches), in pieces cut at the middle of each pause inside the window, none shorter than two seconds and none longer than twelve without a pause in it (v1.152.1: the recogniser's own rough utterance stamps trap the word fitting, so a whole window read again repeats the error, as it did on the office's call; a short piece cannot carry words across a pause) and each piece asked for with the service's `fit_words_over_whole` (v1.153.0, service 0.7.0: the words fitted over the whole piece, so the recogniser's own utterance stamps cannot confine them to the piece's start), and matches the readings' words, together in time order, against the lines it has: a line at least half of whose words are found takes its clock from where those words were heard, in order and never before the line above it; its words, speaker, label, corrections, notes and proposals stay, and its words' own times move with it. A line the reading cannot find keeps its clock and its pill. The re-read is a background pass kept in the Transcript's provenance (waiting, done or failed; polled every ten seconds, given up after half an hour), never a Job of the Queue, so no recording looks as if it were being transcribed again; a re-read that fails leaves the marks. A re-timed line is a sure line: the voice measures it, it may be a Sample, the Preview may seek to it; the block keeps its dotted rule and the pill at its head reads "clock re-read · was 2:05". One audit row, Clock re-read, with the lines moved and the lines left. Undoing a re-read from the kept clock, and asking again for a block whose first pass failed, are named for later.

### The speaker picker (the voice plan's section 13; v1.142.0)

Drawn in `docs/spec/mockups/phase-5-speaker-picker.html` and chosen by the maintainer on 2026-10-20. One control wherever the app asks whose a line or a voice is, and never a typed answer unless the person is new.

- **Three groups, the same everywhere.** **On this call**: the Recording's Speakers as the cards have them, in the cards' order, with colour, number key and a play button that cycles the Speaker's three Samples (1/3, 2/3, 3/3); the Speaker the line has now is struck through and cannot be picked; the Call system is never offered a person's line. **In this case, not yet on this call**: the Case's People with no lines here, with Role, where they appear and their Also called names; their Sample is in another Recording, so play opens the floating Preview (v1.98.0) with that Recording's title. Picking one gives the line to that Person, who appears on the call as a Speaker with a card and a lane. **Someone new**: the name and Role box the cards already have; it makes the Person in the Case and the Speaker on the call in one step, and a name already in the Case joins that Person. Outside a Case the second group is absent and Someone new makes a plain new Speaker.
- **Keys.** The number keys as before, now reaching the Case's People; arrows move the light, Enter picks, Esc closes. A small sheet anchored under the row or the card; on a phone it rises from the foot. While a picker is open the page holds still (v1.142.1): following does not scroll to a sample as it plays, the picker stays under its row, and when it closes the page returns to the line in hand.
- **Beside the card** (v1.143.0; the drawing `docs/spec/mockups/phase-5-speakers-page-breathes.html`, chosen 2026-10-20). On the Speakers page a picker opened from a card (Name or merge..., not this voice, Split here...) floats beside the card at the picker's full width, with a pointer at the card: to the right of the card on a screen 1500 wide or more, under the card over the transcript on a narrower one, kept within the window and placed again as the page scrolls or resizes; on a phone it rises from the foot as before. It never opens inside the card, which is in the page's narrowest column, and never scrolls that column sideways or sits below its fold. The question follows the button: "<Speaker> is" from Name or merge..., with the on-call rows marked "the same person" and the Speaker in hand marked "this one"; "Whose line is this?" with the Sample's words from not this voice; "From <time> the voice is" from Split here..., with "A new speaker on this call" lit. A picker opened from a transcript row stays under its row. Someone new unfolds in place with its name box and Role picker; the number keys and arrows choose a row until something is typed in the box, from when Enter is Name.
- **Where it is.** The Speakers page's "not this speaker" on a row; a Hand-over's Split, which asks whom the lines go to, a known Speaker or Person or a new Speaker (the lit default, so one Enter does what Split did); the recording page's lines, which gain a **speaker** button beside edit and note. The run's proposals keep Accept and Leave. The Case page's Speakers tab's Add person is the same box.
- **The picker only chooses.** A wrong Sample is corrected on the Speakers page's card, where the three Samples live: **not this voice** beside each opens the picker for that line; the move re-picks the Samples and re-scores the pending proposals, with Undo; when none of the three is the voice the card says the label mixes people and points at the Speaker check. Away from the Speakers page the picker carries one quiet line, "Doesn't sound right? Manage speakers".
- **Samples by the voice.** Where the voice step has run, a Speaker's Samples are the lines nearest their voice print, one from each third of the Recording, among lines of a Sample's length where any is; the same choice feeds a proposal's "hear" buttons. Each line keeps its closeness to its Speaker's print as a score (never a voice), written by the check and its re-scoring and cleared when the line changes hands by hand. Where the voice step has not run, chapter 1's length rule stands.

### The voice step and the one run (ADR 0021, 2026-10-19; the voice plan's V2, built in v1.139.0, and V3, built in v1.140.0)

Decided from the measurement in `docs/research/speaker-verification.md`: on the office's recordings the diarizer's labels were wrong on a quarter of judged call lines and a fifth of camera lines, the check's own proposals were right on 3 of 26 the maintainer could judge, and the voice was right on 85 to 100 per cent of the lines it spoke on.

- **The voice's word on every proposal.** As the check runs, the WhisperX service's diarizer container is asked for one vector per line (the line's own words, minus the words the diarizer gave to another label; a mixed line split at the word where the label changes) and one per Speaker. A Speaker's print on the recording is the mean of every line of its label of two seconds or more, outliers dropped and the mean re-taken until it settles. Calibrated on the recording's own lines, each proposal carries **agrees**, **against** or **cannot tell**; cannot tell under the floor (**Voice floor**, per Recording type, 1.5 seconds as shipped) and wherever a print is missing. A proposal the voice is against is shown as such and never in the one-press path; the words "the voice step heard both" and "the voice step was firm" of v1.105.0 give way to these three.
- **The voice's own proposals.** A line far from its own Speaker's print and near another's, that the words never flagged, becomes a correction with the reason "the voice sounds like Speaker 2", under **Voice proposals cap** per run, drawn as the stretch cards are.
- **A Hand-over.** Within one label, two or three voices apart by the recording's own measure that also separate in time are one question: "From 6:12, is this a different person under Speaker 2?", with the moment to hear, **Yes** (the label is split at that time into a new Speaker, with Undo) and **No**. Never proposed where the clusters differ mainly in loudness: a shout is not a second voice.
- **The Call system is carved out.** Its lines (chapter 4 of Phase 1's two-channel call rule, the shipped phrase list) join no print and get no proposal; an announcement spoken over a party's words is split from them at the word. The lines the words find are filled in two ways, neither a setting: a short line, or one beginning with "please", between two system lines within fifteen seconds of each (v1.139.0); and, from v1.161.0, the opening fragments: when the call's first system line starts within the first minute and every line before it is short or begins with "please", each within fifteen seconds of the next, those lines are the system's too; one line that is neither means somebody spoke before the announcement, and nothing before it is taken. A recogniser that cuts lines at pauses (Qwen3-ASR, Phase 1 chapter 7) starts the first announcement with a fragment of its own, which the first rule cannot reach; a mid-call announcement pulls nothing before it. The counts go in the Transcript's provenance.
- **Accept re-scores.** When a person accepts a correction the moved line joins the other Speaker's print and every pending proposal is scored again, so one listened-to decision tidies the rest. The vectors live on the run and are deleted when the check is done; nothing is kept on the Transcript.
- **One run (V3; v1.140.0).** The check and Suggest names become one job, automatic as the Transcript lands or overnight under the check's position, with **Run again** on the page; the names land on the voice cards and the moves and Hand-overs in the transcript, as "The Speakers page" above has it, and from V4 "sounds like" lands on the cards too; a name addressed or introduced by any form of a Person's Also called list reaches the Person with the form as the reason; a suggestion resting on a form two People share is withheld or rated low. The run keeps the name Speaker check; the page's state line says that it also suggests names.
- **Per kind.** The voice step is switched on per Recording type (**Voice step** in the catalogue), shipped on for the kinds the measurement passed and off for the rest; with it off the check runs on words alone, with `voice_of` from the word labels as today.
- **Decided so far** counts per source (the words, the voice, a Hand-over, a name) what people accepted and dismissed, which is the measure after each release.

- **Phase 4 chapter 5, Vision**: the overnight position's window is read by the check too, as the time its day-queued runs wait for. Nothing else in the Vision flow changes; the check is never a stage the batch page or the mail waits for.

## 4. The diarizer

Written 2026-09-24 from the maintainer's decision the same day, after the probe of NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Diarization (`docs/research/nemotron-3-diarization.md`): the only recording the office had corrected proved a bad reference, and by ear the new model was right where the page was wrong on eleven of thirteen disputed stretches, heard voices pyannote had merged into named speakers, and heard silence where the page had words. The maintainer's words: pyannote "was really never that great"; keep its plumbing but turned off; the app is in testing anyway. This chapter makes the model that tells voices apart a choice, ships Nemotron as the default, and keeps pyannote one Panel setting away. Every office that pulls this package gets the same choice, the same default, and no new question at install.

### Principles

1. **The diarizer is a choice, not a fork.** One Panel setting names the model that separates a Recording into Speakers. Whichever is chosen, the service returns the same result shape, so the Transcript, the Speakers page, the Speaker check, the incidents and every export are untouched; a Recording says which diarizer split it.
2. **The default is the better model, and the other stays reachable.** A fresh install diarizes with Nemotron 3 and needs no Hugging Face token for it. pyannote's code, weights and gate stay in the package for an office that prefers it, or for the day the choice is reversed.
3. **Nothing office-specific, nothing new to ask.** The diarizer runs on the same card as the WhisperX service, chosen by the same install question; its weights are fetched by the same `models.yaml`, pinned by revision, offline afterwards; its licence is in the third-party file.
4. **The pinned stack stays pinned.** Nemotron does not go into the WhisperX image. It runs in a container of its own with its own pinned stack, called by the service for the spans, so the stack that took a research note to settle is not disturbed (ADR 0014).

### Words

Added to `CONTEXT.md` with this chapter. **Diarizer**: the model that tells a Recording's voices apart, pyannote or Nemotron; the Panel's word and the Details tab's. The pages never say "engine" for it (the engine is the AI assistant's) and never "model" alone.

### The choice

- **The setting.** Transcription defaults page: **Diarizer**, a choice of `nemotron` ("Nemotron 3 Diarization, NVIDIA, up to eight voices, no token") and `pyannote` ("pyannote community-1, the diarizer until v1.80.0; needs the Hugging Face token given at install"). Default `nemotron`. When changed: the next Run; nothing already transcribed changes. `pyannote` is greyed with "not installed: give the Hugging Face token at install to fetch it" when its weights are not in the cache.
- **Process again** follows the setting as it stands, like every other Run; a Recording split by one diarizer and processed again is split by the current one, names and corrections lost as today.
- **The Speaker-count hint** ("exactly N", "between N and M", on the Upload page and per Side on a call) is pyannote's alone: Nemotron takes no count. Under `nemotron` the Upload page's hint control is greyed with "The Nemotron diarizer decides the count itself", the app sends no hint, and a stored hint on a Recording is kept unused. The recordings table's speaker cells are unchanged.
- **Where it shows.** A Recording's Details tab says "Voices told apart by Nemotron 3 Diarization" or "by pyannote community-1", from the Transcript's provenance; the Word and text exports' processing record gains the same line. The case page and the Speakers page do not name the diarizer.

### The service

- **A second container, `diarizer`**, in the WhisperX service's Compose file, built from this repository as the third image (`ghcr.io/tnmd-fdo/gideon-transcribe-diarizer`, tagged with `RELEASE_TAG` like the other two, its digest recorded and checked as ADR 0013 has it). It holds NeMo's ASR extra at the pinned source commit, torch 2.8.0 with CUDA 12.8 (the WhisperX stack's own, confirmed by the probe on 2026-09-24 to load and run the model with the same output), the model's weights from the shared model cache, and a small HTTP API on the `whisperx` network with no published port. It reserves the same card as the WhisperX service by the same UUID and holds about 2 GB of it while a job diarizes.
- **Its API**, for the WhisperX service alone: `POST /v1/diarize` with the prepared WAV, returning the spans as a list of `{start, end, speaker}` in seconds, with the model's name and version; `GET /healthz`. The Consumer never calls it; the WhisperX service's contract is the only one Consumers hold.
- **A second duty (ADR 0021; the voice plan's V2, not built)**: `POST /v1/voices` with the prepared WAV and a list of stretches (start, end, label), returning one vector per stretch and one per label, from NVIDIA's TitaNet-large (`nvidia/speakerverification_en_titanet_large`, revision pinned, CC-BY-4.0, about 100 MB, trained on telephone and wideband speech) loaded through the same NeMo commit beside Nemotron, under a gigabyte more of the card. Reached by the WhisperX service's contract as a request of its own (`docs/whisperx-api.md`); nothing stored in the container; the service version moves. The measurement that chose the model and its rules is `docs/research/speaker-verification.md`.
- **The WhisperX service's contract** (`docs/whisperx-api.md`, amended with this chapter) gains one request field, `diarizer`: `nemotron` or `pyannote`, default `nemotron` from service 0.3.0. With `nemotron` the service transcribes and aligns as today, asks the diarizer container for the spans, and gives each word its Speaker by WhisperX's own `assign_word_speakers`, so both diarizers attribute words the same way. With `pyannote` nothing changes from today. A `speakers` hint or `return_speaker_embeddings` with `diarizer=nemotron` is refused, `400` with a `reason_class`, under the same rule that refuses a hint without `diarize`: a Consumer that sends one has a bug. The result's `settings_used` names the diarizer and its version; `speakers.labels` are `speaker_0`, `speaker_1` and on for Nemotron, which the app renames to Speaker 1 and on as it does pyannote's `SPEAKER_00`.
- **The pins.** `models.yaml` gains a `diarizer` entry: `nvidia/Nemotron-3-Diarization`, revision pinned, licence OpenMDW-1.1, not gated, size recorded; `pull` fetches it into the shared cache like the others and the diarizer container reads it offline. pyannote's entry stays; when no Hugging Face token is given, `pull` skips the gated model, says so, and the Panel greys the `pyannote` choice. With the second duty (V2) `models.yaml` gains a `voices` entry for TitaNet-large by revision, fetched and checked the same way, and `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md` its attribution.
- **The diarizer's own settings**, in the service's environment file with the WhisperX service's fixed rules: the offline chunking configuration from the model card (spkcache 264, fifo 40, chunk 340, right context 40, update period 300 frames of 80 ms) and the batch size. Not Panel settings.
- **The benchmark gate** runs once on the server before the tag, as for every model change: the diarizer's time per hour of audio and card memory, written into the research note.

### In and out

- The exports' processing record gains the diarizer's line; nothing else is exported differently. Nothing is imported.
